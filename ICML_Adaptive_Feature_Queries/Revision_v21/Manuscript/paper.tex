\begin{abstract}
Questionnaires, laboratory panels and budgeted classifiers reveal at most $b$ of $m$ features per record. If each record's $b$ questions must be fixed in advance instead of chosen from its earlier answers, how many more records does it take to estimate a known low-degree functional, such as the accuracy of a frozen screening rule? We show that pre-committed question sets simulate any adaptive protocol exactly, in law, with $2^{b-1}$ times the records in expectation, whatever the task and the population, and that this factor is attained when there are enough features. The worst case is robust to relaxations: with $r$ rounds of adaptivity the simulation costs $2^{b-r}$ and a constant number of rounds can still lose $2^{b(1-o(1))}$; $c$ extra pre-committed questions per record divide the worst-case price by at most $c+1$; and a mean budget instead of a hard cap by at most $b$, when there are exponentially many features. The price belongs to targeted collection: for all $b$-way interactions at once, uniform forms are optimal even among branching designs. A certified solver computes the optimal design of each kind for a given rule and population. \nAbstractData
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Population quantities are often estimated from records that each reveal only a few features. Questionnaires cap respondent burden, clinical protocols cap the tests per patient, and budgeted classifiers acquire a few inputs per case \citep{turney1995,trapeznikov2013,nan2017}. The collector can pre-commit to each record's feature set, as in split questionnaires and laboratory panels ordered at once \citep{raghunathan1995,chipperfield2009}, or choose each question after seeing earlier answers, as in skip logic, adaptive matrix sampling \citep{gonzalez2008} and reflex testing. In machine-learning terms, the first is a static feature subset drawn per example and the second an adaptive feature-acquisition policy with a hard budget \citep{covert2023}; here the goal is not a prediction for the example but an estimate for the population, such as the accuracy of a frozen rule or the accuracy gain of a model update. Two settings make the constraints concrete. In large assessments and health surveys the outcome is on the frame or in a registry, session length caps the items per respondent, and booklets are fixed before fieldwork; multistage adaptive forms were introduced to relax exactly this \citep{yamamoto2018}. In on-device evaluation of a deployed rule the outcome is logged centrally, the features stay on the device, a privacy or communication budget caps how many a device discloses, and a device answers one pre-specified query, because interactive protocols need it to stay available across rounds \citep{joseph2019,kairouz2021}.

This paper asks what pre-commitment costs in records. That adaptivity within one input can be worth exponentially many queries is classical in decision-tree complexity, the huge-object model and interactive local privacy \citep{goldreich2022,adar2024,joseph2019}. Under a per-record cap the price is paid in records, and it is not known what it is exactly, how robust it is to small relaxations, or what it is for rules that are actually trained. We answer these three questions.

\textbf{The exact price (Section~\ref{sec:gap}).} Pre-committed forms reproduce any adaptive protocol exactly in law, for every estimator and loss, with $K_{m,b}=\min\{2^{b-1},\binom{m-1}{b-1}\}$ times the records in expectation. This is the folklore conversion of adaptive into non-adaptive queries made exact across records; the new part is that $2^{b-1}$ is attained, for a single linear functional and whatever the accuracy of the rule, by an address contrast on $b-1+2^{b-1}$ features.

\textbf{Its robustness (Section~\ref{sec:gap}).} Neither a few rounds of adaptivity, nor a few extra questions, nor a mean budget removes the worst case. With $r$ rounds the simulation costs $2^{b-r}$, and a pointer tree loses at least $2^{b-1}/(1+2b/r)^r$, which is $2^{b(1-o(1))}$ for constant $r$; the two bounds do not match beyond that. Forms with $c<2^{b-1}$ extra questions need between $2^{b-1}/(c+1)$ and $2^{b+1}/(c+3)$ times the records in the worst case, and in polynomial dimension forms twice as long as the rule's paths still lose $2^{b(1-o(1))}$.

\textbf{The price at a population, computed with certificates (Sections~\ref{sec:general}--\ref{sec:model}).} The minimax constant of each protocol class is a convex program, and a column-generation solver with exact pricing over trees finds the optimal design of every class. A working model for the unasked features leaves the simulation bound intact, removes the gap up to a factor two when the features are independent given the label, and does not remove it under strong couplings.

\textbf{What it is for trained rules (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).} \nContribData

The setting is deliberately simple: every sampled record's label is observed and charged separately, the target is one known contrast of degree at most $b$ on binary features, and answers do not depend on question order. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses what this leaves out and what a practitioner can take from the results.

\section{Estimand and Acquisition Protocols}\label{sec:setup}
Let $X$ take values in $\X=\{-1,1\}^m$ and let $Y\in\{-1,1\}$ be a label. Freeze two rules $p_0,p_1:\X\to[0,1]$ that predict $+1$ with probabilities $p_j(x)$, put $d=p_1-p_0$, $t(x,y)=yd(x)$, and
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(P)=\E_P[t(X,Y)]=\E_P[Yd(X)].\label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
This is the accuracy difference of the two rules; $p_0\equiv1/2$ makes it affine in the accuracy of $p_1$ alone (sensitivity, specificity and other proper scores: Appendix~\ref{app:metrics}). The cap $b$ bounds the feature queries per record and is \emph{binding} when $m>b$.

\paragraph{Protocols.}
An interview $\iota$ maps a profile $x$ to a transcript $\ell_\iota(x)$. A \emph{fixed form} queries a set of coordinates chosen before the record is seen. An \emph{$r$-round interview} queries batches $B_1,B_2(x_{B_1}),\ldots,B_r$, each chosen from the answers to earlier batches, with $\sum_s|B_s|\le b$. A \emph{branching interview} is a decision tree of depth at most $b$. The menus are nested, $\A_{\rm fixed}=\A_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\A_b=\A_{\rm branch}$. In machine-learning terms an interview is a per-example acquisition policy, a form is a static feature subset, and a collection of records under a design is an evaluation run, which we call an audit. In missing-data terms a fixed form is missingness completely at random by design, and the other interviews are randomized monotone coarsenings \citep{gill1997}, which are ignorable. Across records a protocol may use all previous records to choose the next interview. $R_\A(d,n)$ is the minimax mean squared error over protocols and estimators with $n$ records and interviews from $\A$.

\paragraph{Identification.}
The contrast is identifiable for all populations exactly when $d$ is a sum of functions that are each constant on the transcript cells of one interview; for forms of size $b$ and for depth-$b$ trees this is the same condition, Fourier degree $\deg(d)\le b$ in the characters $\chi_S(x)=\prod_{j\in S}x_j$. It holds for every probability average of depth-$b$ trees, such as the predicted probability of a depth-limited random forest, and fails for most thresholded ensembles at small caps.

\paragraph{Designs and constants.}
A \emph{design} draws an interview $\iota$ with probability $q_\iota$ and scores its transcript with a table $g_\iota$; the score $Yg_\iota(\ell_\iota(X))/q_\iota$ is unbiased for $Yd(X)$ at every profile exactly when $\sum_\iota g_\iota(\ell_\iota(x))=d(x)$ for all $x$. Define the worst-case and population constants
\begin{align}
 V(d,\A)&=\inf_{q,g:\ \sum_\iota g_\iota=d}\ \max_{x\in\X}\sum_\iota\frac{g_\iota(\ell_\iota(x))^2}{q_\iota},\label{eq:unbiasedprogram}\\
 W_{\A}(P)&=\inf_{q,H:\,\E[H\mid X,Y]=t(X,Y)}\Var_{P,q}(H),\label{eq:mainW}
\end{align}
where $H$ is a score of the drawn interview's transcript and the label that may be tuned to $P$. Thus $V$ is the worst-case and $W_\A(P)$ the population price of estimating $\Delta$ from interviews in $\A$, in units of variance per record; Theorem~\ref{thm:char} shows that they are the first-order minimax risks, and ratios of constants are ratios of records needed.

\paragraph{A worked example.}
Let $m=3$, $b=2$ and $d(x)=[(1+x_1)x_2+(1-x_1)x_3]/2$: the first answer selects which of two others is the target. Branching reads $x_1$ and then the selected coordinate, so it observes $t$. Under uniform profiles and an independent uniform label, the best pre-committed design draws $\{1,2\}$ or $\{1,3\}$ with probability $1/2$ and doubles the score when the drawn form contains the selected coordinate: it needs twice the records.

\section{The Price of Pre-Commitment}\label{sec:gap}
\begin{theorem}[Exact simulation and the sharp gap]\label{thm:universal}
Let $1\le b\le m$ and $K_{m,b}=\min\{2^{b-1},\binom{m-1}{b-1}\}$.
\textup{(i)} Every protocol on $n$ branching records, with any estimator, is reproduced with the same law at every population by a fixed-form protocol on a random number of records with mean $K_{m,b}n$.
\textup{(ii)} Consequently, for every contrast of degree at most $b$, $V_{\rm fixed}\le K_{m,b}V_{\rm branch}$, and at every law $P$ of $(X,Y)$,
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm branch}(P)\le W_{\rm fixed}(P)\le K_{m,b}\,W_{\rm branch}(P).\label{eq:universal}
\end{equation}
\textup{(iii)} The address contrast on $m=b-1+2^{b-1}$ coordinates has $V_{\rm fixed}/V_{\rm branch}=2^{b-1}$, and $W_{\rm fixed}(P)/W_{\rm branch}(P)=2^{b-1}$ at uniform profiles whenever the label agrees with the rule with a probability in $(0,1)$ that does not depend on the profile. With $m\le b^2$ there are examples with ratio at least $2^{b-b/(\lfloor\log_2 b\rfloor+1)-\lfloor\log_2 b\rfloor}$.
\end{theorem}

\caption{The three constructions behind Theorems~\ref{thm:universal} and~\ref{thm:rounds}. (a) Address contrast: $\kappa=b-1$ address bits select one of $D=2^\kappa$ data bits; branching reads the address and then the selected bit, while any fixed set of $b$ coordinates explains at most a fraction $1/D$ of the target's variance under the uniform law. (b) Path guessing: a fixed form draws one of a tree's at most $2^{b-1}$ padded path sets and keeps the record when the path completes inside it. (c) Binary pointer tree: $L=b-1$ levels of pointer bits select a child and the target is the data bit at the reached leaf; a round that reads $a$ pointer bits raises the conditional probability of the most likely leaf by at most a factor $1+2a$ in expectation.}
\label{fig:constructions}
\end{figure*}

\begin{proof}
\emph{(i)} Remove repeated questions from a tree $\tau$ and pad every root-to-leaf path, by a fixed rule, with unused coordinates to exactly $b$ distinct ones. All path sets contain the root question, and the first $b-1$ answers determine the set, so the collection $\mathcal S_\tau$ of distinct path sets has at most $K_{m,b}$ elements. To simulate one record of $\tau$, draw one set of $\mathcal S_\tau$ uniformly and ask it as a form (Figure~\ref{fig:constructions}b). The answers reveal whether the drawn set is this record's own padded path set (follow the tree while its questions lie in the set), and for every profile exactly one set is; so the check succeeds with probability $1/|\mathcal S_\tau|$ whatever the record is. Accept a success with probability $|\mathcal S_\tau|/K_{m,b}$. An accepted record is then a draw from the population together with the transcript that $\tau$ would have produced, and the number of records used until acceptance is geometric with mean $K_{m,b}$ and independent of that transcript. The tree may depend on earlier transcripts, so the argument applies record by record to any protocol (Theorem~\ref{thm:simulation}). \emph{(ii)} For $V$, apply a near-optimal branching design to the records accepted from a fixed budget, whose number is binomial with success probability $1/K_{m,b}$. At a given $P$ an explicit score suffices: draw a tree of the branching design and one of its path sets, and return $c+|\mathcal S_\tau|(H-c)$ if the set is the record's own and $c$ otherwise, where $H$ is the tree's score and $c=\Delta(P)$. This score has the conditional mean of $H$ at every profile and at most $K_{m,b}$ times its variance (Appendix~\ref{app:population}). \emph{(iii)} Let $\kappa=b-1$ address bits select one of $D=2^\kappa$ data bits, $d(A,Z)=Z_A$ (Figure~\ref{fig:constructions}a). Branching computes $d$. Under the uniform law, a form $T$ with $s$ address bits and $w\ge1$ data bits has $s\le\kappa+1-w$ and explains the energy
\begin{equation}
 \|\E[d\mid X_T]\|_2^2=w\,2^{s-2\kappa}\le2^{-\kappa},\label{eq:addressinformation}
\end{equation}
because each of its data bits is consistent with a fraction $2^{-s}$ of the partial addresses and is then the selected bit with conditional probability $2^{-(\kappa-s)}$. The dual of Theorem~\ref{thm:char} with the uniform law and the witness $h=d$ compares $\|d\|_2^4=1$ with this energy, so $V_{\rm fixed}\ge D$. The same energy bounds the variance that a form explains when the label is a noisy copy of $d$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:labelnoise}), and products of address blocks give the example in polynomial dimension (Appendix~\ref{app:productaddress}).
\end{proof}
Part (i) is the folklore conversion of an adaptive into a non-adaptive query procedure, carried out across records and made exact by the acceptance step; what the theorem adds is that the resulting constant is attained, already for one linear functional. Our sharp example uses $m=b-1+2^{b-1}$ features; for $m\le b^2$ we only have the product examples, and for smaller $m$ the extreme ratio is open (at $m=4$, $b=3$ it lies between $2.457$ and $K_{4,3}=3$; Proposition~\ref{prop:fourbit}). \emph{One target or many.} The simulation involves no target, so the upper bounds hold for any family of targets and for rules chosen after collection, and the lower bound extends, for the worst member, to families that contain the address contrast and consist of functions of the address tree's transcript, such as all relabelings of its leaves. At the other extreme the price vanishes: for all monomials of degree $b$ at once, uniform forms are minimax among all designs with unbiased scores, branching included, because every record yields exactly one question set however it is chosen (Proposition~\ref{prop:family}). Adaptivity pays when the collector knows which questions the target needs; a multipurpose collection should use forms.

\begin{theorem}[Rounds and extra questions]\label{thm:rounds}
Let $K_r=\min\{2^{b-r},\binom{m-r}{b-r}\}$ and let $V_r$, $W_r(P)$ be the constants of $r$-round interviews.
\textup{(a)} Part \textup{(i)} of Theorem~\ref{thm:universal} holds for $r$-round interviews with $K_r$ in place of $K_{m,b}$; hence $V_r\le K_rV_{\rm branch}$ and $W_r(P)\le K_rW_{\rm branch}(P)$ at every $P$.
\textup{(b)} The binary pointer tree on $m=2^b-1$ coordinates has $V_{\rm branch}=1$ and $V_r\ge2^{b-1}/(1+2b/r)^{r}$ for $1\le r\le b$; a product of pointer trees on $m\le b^2$ coordinates has $\log_2V_r\ge b-O(rb\log\log b/\log b)$.
\textup{(c)} For $b+c\le m$, forms of size $b+c$ reproduce every protocol on depth-$b$ interviews with $K^{+}_{b,c}=2^{b-1-j(c)}$ times the records in expectation, where $j(c)$ is the largest $j\le b-1$ with $2^{j+1}-j-2\le c$, so that $K^{+}_{b,c}<2^{b+1}/(c+3)$ whenever $K^{+}_{b,c}>1$. The address contrast needs exactly $2^{b-1}/(c+1)$ times the records for $c<2^{b-1}$, and a product of address blocks on $m\le b^2$ features needs $2^{b(1-o(1))}$ for every $c=O(b)$.
\end{theorem}
For (a), follow the tree for $r-1$ rounds and guess the rest of the path in the last batch.

\emph{Sketch of (b).} By the dual of Theorem~\ref{thm:char} with the uniform law and $h=d$, $V_r\ge1/\max_\iota\E[\E[d\mid\ell_\iota]^2]$. Only a data bit that the interview has read carries signal, so the energy of $\iota$ is at most $\E\pi_r$, where $\pi_t$ is the conditional probability, given the transcript of $t$ rounds, that the data bit of the selected leaf has been read (Figure~\ref{fig:constructions}c). Let $M_t$ be $2^{b-1}$ times the largest conditional probability of a leaf given the transcript of $t$ rounds, and $Q_t=M_t+2^{b-1}\pi_t$, so that $Q_0=1$. A round that reads $a$ pointer bits and $\lambda$ data bits satisfies $\E[Q_t\mid\text{past}]\le(1+2a+\lambda)Q_{t-1}$: each pointer bit read adds at most $2M_{t-1}$ to $M$ in expectation, because a chain of $j$ newly read bits multiplies a leaf's probability by $2^j$ only if the first $j-1$ point the right way, and each data bit adds at most the current largest leaf probability to $\pi$. With at most $b$ questions in $r$ rounds, $2^{b-1}\E\pi_r\le\E Q_r\le(1+2b/r)^r$. The potential is multiplicative over independent blocks, which gives the product statement (Appendix~\ref{app:roundshier}).

The two rounds bounds do not match. They agree up to a polynomial in $b$ for constant $r$; on the pointer tree a protocol within a polynomial factor of branching needs at least $0.37b-O(\log b)$ rounds; in polynomial dimension the lower bound covers only $r=o(\log b/\log\log b)$, since the product is evaluated exactly in $\lfloor\log_2b\rfloor$ rounds; and at $b=3,4$ the pointer tree loses $1.80$ and between $3.32$ and $3.33$ in two rounds against the upper bounds $2$ and $4$ (Appendix~\ref{app:pointerexact}), so we do not know a contrast that attains $2^{b-r}$ for $r\ge2$.

For (c), guess only the first $b-1-j$ answers and put the whole subtree below the guessed node into the form, which costs $2^{j+1}-j-2$ extra questions; for the address contrast, a form with all address bits and $c+1$ data bits explains a fraction $(c+1)/2^{b-1}$ of the variance and no form explains more (Appendix~\ref{app:augment}). Extra questions thus buy at most a factor linear in their number. The same holds for a mean budget: if records may ask up to $b+c$ features but only $b$ on average, the worst-case price is between $\frac{b+c}{b}\cdot2^{b-1}/(c+1)$ and $\frac{b+c}{b}K^{+}_{b,c}$, at least $2^{b-1}/b$ for every $c<2^{b-1}$; under a pure mean budget it is at most $m/b$ (ask all features with probability $b/m$), which is small in polynomial dimension and still about $2^{b-1}/b$ for the address contrast (Theorem~\ref{thm:augment}(d), Proposition~\ref{prop:hybrid}).

\section{Constants and Their Computation}\label{sec:general}\label{sec:population}
The simulation bounds the gap; the constants $V$ and $W$ of Section~\ref{sec:setup} measure it for a given contrast and population. Let $\sigma_P^2=\Var_P(t)$; for a depth-$b$ tree $\tau$ that computes $d$, with padded path sets $S_\tau(x)$, let $\rho_P^2=\max_{|T|=b}\Var_P(\E_P[t\mid X_T,Y])/\sigma_P^2$ be the largest share of the target's variance that one form explains, and $N_P(\tau)=(\sum_Sv_S^{1/2})^2$ with $v_S=\E_P[(t-\Delta)^2\mathbf 1\{S_\tau(X)=S\}]/\sigma_P^2$ the effective number of question sets the tree uses at $P$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact constants]\label{thm:char}\label{thm:bracket}
Let $\A$ be a finite menu on which $d$ is identifiable.
\textup{(a)} $nR_\A(d,n)\to V(d,\A)$; for every $\epsilon>0$ an iid design with an unbiased score has worst-case risk at most $(V+\epsilon)/n$ for every $n$, and the converse covers biased estimators, designs that adapt across records and interview choices that depend on the current label. Moreover $V(d,\A)=\sup_{\mu,h}\langle d,h\rangle_\mu^2/\max_{\iota\in\A}\|\E_\mu[h\mid\ell_\iota(X)]\|_\mu^2$ over positive profile laws $\mu$ and witnesses $h$.
\textup{(b)} At a full-support law $P$, $W_\A(P)/n$ is the local asymptotic minimax risk over arbitrary estimators from the capped records, for interviews drawn without the record's label; drawing them with the label can only lower the constant (Proposition~\ref{prop:labeldesign}).
\textup{(c)} If a depth-$b$ tree $\tau$ computes $d$ and $\sigma_P^2>0$, then $W_{\rm branch}(P)=\sigma_P^2$ and
\[
 \frac1{\rho_P^2}\ \le\ \frac{W_{\rm fixed}(P)}{W_{\rm branch}(P)}\ \le\ N_P(\tau)\ \le\ K_{m,b},
\]
with equality throughout for the address contrast under the uniform law. For every menu, constant $c$ and witness $h$ (with $0/0=0$), $W_\A(P)+(\Delta-c)^2\ge\E_P[(t-c)h]^2/\max_{\iota\in\A}\E_P[\E_P[h\mid\ell_\iota(X),Y]^2]$.
\end{theorem}
The ingredients are standard: minimax duality for linear functionals \citep{donoho1994,juditsky2009,polyanskiy2026} and, for one menu, $c$-optimal design with the efficient augmented inverse-probability score under known coarsening \citep{elfving1952,pukelsheim1993,robins1994,tsiatis2006}. What is new is the comparison across menus and the exact optimization over the combinatorial menus of $r$-round and branching interviews. Ratios of $W$ are ratios of records needed when $n$ is large and each class uses its best design for $P$ (Appendices~\ref{app:B}, \ref{app:localminimax}, \ref{app:population}).

\paragraph{Computing every protocol class.}
Pad every transcript to exactly $b$ coordinates, so that an interview is a partition of the cube into cells $\{x:x_T=a\}$ with $|T|=b$. Pointwise unbiasedness of the tables is then equivalent to matching the Fourier coefficients of $t$ of degree at most $b$, and \eqref{eq:mainW} over a list of interviews is a second-order cone program in the tables and the interview probabilities. Column generation solves a restricted master problem over a few interviews; for its dual witness, the most informative interview of any class $\A_r$ is found by dynamic programming over partial assignments. The witness certifies a lower bound through the last display of Theorem~\ref{thm:bracket}, and the master value is an upper bound. Pricing visits $O(\sum_{k\le b}\binom mk4^k)$ partial assignments per iteration and never touches the $2^m$ profiles (Appendix~\ref{app:engine}).

\paragraph{The estimator in practice.} For a rule given as a tree $\tau$, the menu of $\tau$'s own padded path sets, at most $2^{b-1}$ forms whatever $m$ is, gives the \emph{path-set program}: a convex program with at most $2^{b-1}\cdot2^{b+1}$ score variables whose optimum is a certified upper bound on $W_{\rm fixed}(P)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:upper}). Its inputs are the cell masses of the path sets, estimated from reference data or from a pilot; its outputs are form probabilities $q_S$ and tables $g_S$. The audit draws a form $S_i\sim q$ for record $i$, asks it, and reports the mean of $Y_ig_{S_i}(X_{i,S_i})/q_{S_i}$ with a standard error from the sample variance. The unbiasedness identities hold at every profile, so the estimate stays exactly unbiased when the reference is wrong; only its variance depends on it (Proposition~\ref{prop:selfcal}).

\section{What a Working Model Changes}\label{sec:model}
The constants so far assume nothing about the unasked features. If the law of $(X,Y)$ is assumed to lie in a smooth parametric model with tangent space $\mathcal T$ at $P$, the constant of a menu \emph{within the model} is $W^{\mathcal T}_\A(P)=\inf_q\sup_{h\in\mathcal T}\langle t-\Delta,h\rangle_P^2/\sum_\iota q_\iota J_\iota(h)$ with $J_\iota(h)=\E_P[\E_P[h\mid\ell_\iota(X),Y]^2]$, the $c$-optimal design value for the model's scores (Appendix~\ref{app:structured}); it is a lower bound on the local minimax risk within the model, attained when the optimal design has nonsingular information, and the same solver computes it.

\begin{theorem}[Adaptivity in a model]\label{thm:structured}
\textup{(a)} In every model, at every law $P$ and for $1\le r\le b$: $W^{\mathcal T}_r(P)\le K_r\,W^{\mathcal T}_{\rm branch}(P)$.
\textup{(b)} In the model $\mathcal M_{\rm ci}$ in which the features are independent given the label, at every law of the model and for every contrast: fixed forms, $r$-round and branching interviews have the same constant if the design may depend on the record's label, and $W^{\rm ci}_{\rm fixed}(P)\le2\,W^{\rm ci}_{\rm branch}(P)$ if it may not.
\textup{(c)} For every $b\ge3$ and $\epsilon>0$ there are full-support laws in the pairwise log-linear model, on the $b-1+2^{b-1}$ features of the address contrast and $2^b$ auxiliary features, at which the constants of that contrast within the pairwise model, and within every log-linear model of higher order, satisfy $W^{(k)}_{\rm fixed}/W^{(k)}_{\rm branch}\ge(1-\epsilon)\,2^{b-1}/b$.
\end{theorem}
Part (a) is the simulation of Theorem~\ref{thm:universal}, which involves no model. Part (b) is the benign extreme, and it holds at rules of any accuracy: when the features are independent given the label, as in a naive-Bayes or latent-class model, a record informs the estimate only through \emph{which} coordinates it reveals, and the constant of a design is $\Var(\E[t\mid Y])+\sum_yP(y)\sum_j(\hat d^{\,y}_j)^2/\pi_j(y)$, where $\pi_j(y)$ is the probability that feature $j$ is asked of a record with label $y$ and $\hat d^{\,y}_j$ is the degree-one coefficient of the rule in class $y$. Whatever inclusion probabilities a tree realizes, a label-dependent mixture of forms realizes as well; without the label a tree can use early answers as a proxy for it, which is worth at most a factor two. Part (c) shows that parsimony alone is not enough: with a one-hot copy of the address and the products of its indicators with the data bits, tied by strong pairwise couplings, the address contrast is a polynomial of degree two on a set of probability nearly one; it is the address lower bound in another coding, and it shows that the order of a log-linear model measures parsimony only relative to the coding of the features. Between the two extremes we have bounds only at and near product laws, where the gap under a log-linear model of order $k$ is at most $(b+1)^k$ at uniform marginals, and none in between (Theorem~\ref{thm:structuredfull}); Section~\ref{sec:experiments} computes the constants there.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\emph{Adaptivity in query models.} Within-input adaptivity is classical in decision-tree and property-testing complexity, with round hierarchies \citep{canonne2018,li2026regex} and pointer chasing \citep{nisan1993}. That a $q$-query adaptive procedure is simulated by $2^q$ non-adaptive queries to the same input is folklore there; the textbook non-adaptive estimator for a decision tree samples its Fourier coefficients \citep{kushilevitz1993} and has second moment $\|\hat d\|_1^2$, which is $4^{b-1}$ for the address contrast, against $2^{b-1}$ here. The locally bounded model of \citet{adar2024}, with its exponential separation from non-adaptive testers \citep{goldreich2022,chakraborty2023}, is the testing counterpart of our branching class. Adaptivity gaps are also studied in stochastic optimization \citep{dean2008,golovin2011}, batched bandits \citep{perchet2016,gao2019} and adaptive sensing \citep{ariascastro2013}.
\emph{Information-constrained estimation} fixes a per-sample channel family \citep{acharya2019,barnes2020,acharya2022tit,lau2026}; pointer chasing separates rounds of interaction in local privacy \citep{joseph2019,joseph2020}, and interactive protocols estimate some functionals at faster rates \citep{butucea2023}. Ours is a noiseless analogue with exact constants.
\emph{Design and missing data by design.} For one menu our programs are $c$-optimal design and our solver a vertex-direction method \citep{fedorov1972,wynn1970,sagnol2011,stenger1979,gabler1990,aronow2026}. Split questionnaires and matrix sampling assign fixed forms \citep{raghunathan1995,graham2006,thomas2006,chipperfield2009,rhemtulla2012}, optimized under a model from a pilot \citep{adiguzel2008,jang2022}; adaptive matrix sampling \citep{gonzalez2008}, responsive design \citep{groves2006}, two-phase designs \citep{reilly1995,gilbert2014,mcisaac2015,tao2020,chen2020,chen2022} and multistage adaptive tests \citep{yamamoto2018,fink2024} are two-round interviews in our sense. These lines optimize one protocol within one design space; we compare the spaces.
\emph{Evaluation and acquisition in ML.} Restricted-focus-of-attention learning is our fixed-form class \citep{bendavid1998}, and budgeted learning fits predictors from such views \citep{cesa2011,hazan2012,lizotte2003,kapoor2005}. Active testing and prediction-powered inference allocate labels \citep{kossen2021,zrnic2024,angelopoulos2023}, the reverse of our cost structure; active feature acquisition chooses features for individual predictions \citep{trapeznikov2013,nan2017,covert2023,vonkleist2025}.

\section*{Impact Statement}
Shorter adaptive questionnaires and test sequences can reduce respondent and patient burden. Branching exposes people to different questions; order effects, selective nonresponse and subgroup-specific burdens can bias estimates or hide unequal outcomes, and population-average gains do not imply subgroup gains, so deployments should monitor both.
