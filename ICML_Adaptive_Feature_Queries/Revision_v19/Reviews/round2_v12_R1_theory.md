**Summary**

The paper studies estimation of a linear population functional Δ(P) = E_P[Y d(X)] (e.g., the accuracy of a frozen classifier, or the accuracy gain of a model update, with d = p1 − p0) when every sampled record reveals its label but at most b of its m binary features. It compares protocol classes at their minimax optimum: pre-committed "fixed forms" (a b-subset chosen before seeing the record), r-round batched interviews, and fully "branching" interviews (depth-b decision trees). Theorem 1 gives the exact first-order minimax constant V(d, A) for any finite menu A as a finite convex program (a min over design/score tables of the worst-profile second moment), with a dual in terms of the largest projection energy a single interview captures; the converse holds for biased estimators, across-record adaptation and label-peeking interview choice, via a change of measure, KL chain rule and a van Trees argument. Theorem 2 shows the worst-case fixed/branching ratio is exactly min{2^{b−1}, C(m−1,b−1)} for contrasts of Fourier degree ≤ b, tight at an address/indexing contrast; extensions cover finite n, s-ary alphabets, polynomial dimension m ≤ b² (ratio 2^{b−O(b/log b)}) and mean budgets (ratio ≤ m/b). Theorem 3 gives a round hierarchy: r rounds lose at most 2^{b−r}, while a pointer-chasing contrast separates r from r+1 rounds by 2^{⌊(b−1)/r⌋}/b, so two rounds can be exponentially worse than full branching. Theorem 4 brackets the population-specific second-moment ratio between 1/Γ_P (inverse of the energy fraction explained by the best single form) and the Rényi-1/2 effective number of path sets of a tree computing d. An exact column-generation algorithm with a dynamic-programming pricing step computes the branching constant over all depth-b trees (≈1.5×10^7 trees for m=12, b=3) with certified bounds. Experiments on retrained random-forest updates (median ratio 1.51, max 1.85), BRFSS/thyroid screening trees (ratios 1.00–1.98), multiple imputation comparisons, and a PISA planned-missing archive illustrate the theory.

**Strengths**

- Natural, cleanly posed model that bridges two literatures usually kept apart: adaptive vs. non-adaptive query complexity (decision trees, huge-object model, round hierarchies) and planned-missingness survey design. The reformulation "under a hard per-record cap the price of non-adaptivity is paid in records, not queries" is crisp and, to my knowledge, has not been worked out before in this estimation form.
- The main theorems are correct. I verified: the Sion/Fenchel duality and the van Trees converse of Theorem 1 (A11.1–A11.3), including the handling of label-dependent interview choice; the padding argument (first b−1 answers determine the path's coordinate set) and the Horvitz–Thompson simulation in A12.1–A12.2; the Fourier/projection-energy computation r·2^{s−2κ} ≤ 2^{−κ} for the address contrast (A12.3); the "generous transcript / deep query / hit" argument for the pointer-chasing lower bound (A3, Steps 1–3), which is careful about measurability of the unrevealed register address and the union bound over at most b queries; the Cauchy–Schwarz lower bound and the Rényi-1/2 optimization in Theorem 4; the product-of-blocks inequality in A20.1 and the hybrid-budget bound (23) in A21.1.
- Theorem 2 is genuinely sharp in both directions and comes with a complete set of satellites (finite-n visibility threshold n ≥ 60D in Prop. 8, s-ary alphabets, polynomial dimension, mean vs. hard budget interpolation with an exact formula DB/[b(B−κ)]). The observation that across-record adaptation and early access to the current label do not change the first-order constant, while within-record adaptation changes it exponentially, is a clean conceptual message.
- The round hierarchy (Theorem 3) is new in this setting and its proof is self-contained and readable.
- The population-specific bracket is simple but interpretable (effective number of paths vs. energy explained by one form), and the paper shows it actually predicts the empirical outcomes (thyroid: Γ_P = 1, no gain).
- The exact computation over all depth-3 trees via column generation with an O(m^b 2^b) DP pricing step is a real computational contribution, with certified gaps ≤ 10^{−6}, an independent re-implementation, and released code/certificates. The empirical finding that menus restricted to the forests' own component trees capture only ~17% of the log-gain is informative.
- The experimental reporting is honest: gains are modest (1–2×), some settings show no gain, fitted designs lose 3–7% vs oracles, imputation can match branching when transport holds, and the PISA archive is explicitly labeled as having no branching counterfactual.

**Weaknesses**

1. [major] Limited technical novelty/depth relative to known adaptivity separations. Each core ingredient is the textbook one: the 2^{b−1} bound is the standard random-path Horvitz–Thompson simulation (the classical D^{na} ≤ 2^{D} − 1 simulation sharpened by one bit), the lower bound uses the indexing/address function that is the standard tight example for adaptive vs. non-adaptive decision-tree complexity, the round hierarchy uses pointer chasing exactly as in Nisan–Wigderson / Canonne–Gur, and Theorem 1 is (as the authors acknowledge) an application of Donoho/Juditsky–Nemirovski/Polyanskiy–Wu-type linear-functional duality plus van Trees. An expert would anticipate both the answer 2^{b−1} and its proof. The contribution is the model and the exact constants, not new techniques.
2. [major] Scope of the estimand is restrictive, and the abstract/introduction oversell it. Identification requires deg(d) ≤ b, which holds for shallow trees and for probability-output averages of depth-≤ b trees, but not for thresholded ensembles, GBDTs or MLPs (A26 certifies positive floors for these). The headline phrase "the accuracy of a frozen classifier" thus applies only to a narrow model class; for everything else the paper offers only a crude bias-plus-V/n bound (A13.3), not a rate-matching analysis of the biased regime.
3. [major] Significance of the empirical gains. The worst-case theory promises 2^{b−1} (up to 32× at b = 6), but on real models the exact gain is a median 1.5× and at most 1.98×; in several settings it is exactly 1. The 25× figure in the abstract is against uniform path guessing with completion-only scores, i.e., a strawman that no survey methodologist would deploy once augmented IPW scores exist (Robins–Rotnitzky–Zhao). The paper's own optimized fixed-form scores remove most of that; what remains is the modest intrinsic part. This weakens the practical case for the paper's central question.
4. [minor] The round-hierarchy results have a wide gap and a vacuous range. Lower bound exponent ⌊(b−1)/r⌋ − log₂ b versus upper bound b − r; the factor b in D/b comes from a union bound and is very likely slack (the natural r-round protocol achieves exactly D). Corollary 12's bound 2^{⌊(b−1)/2⌋}/b is < 1 for b ≤ 6, so "for every b ≥ 3" is literally true but empty for small b; the main text should say so. The pointer-chasing instance also needs m exponential in r (D^r data coordinates), which the main text does not mention.
5. [minor] Mismatch between Theorem 4 and what is reported. The bracket [1/Γ_P, N_P] bounds the second-moment ratio M_fixed/M_branch, whereas Table 2/3 report the variance ratio W_fixed/W_branch (with Δ ≈ 0.13–0.35 and E t² = 1/4 for deterministic screening, the two differ noticeably). The claim "every run lies inside the bracket" is therefore only partly implied by the theorem (the lower end follows since W-ratio ≥ M-ratio; the upper end does not).
6. [minor] Presentation. The abstract is a wall of numbers; the main body stops at ~6.5 pages yet defers nearly all intuition (why the dual has the form it has, why the first b−1 answers determine the set, what the "hit" argument is doing) to a 47-page appendix with 32 sections, part of which (A8) explicitly retains "experiments of the previous version of the study". The universal constant 104 is an odd thing to feature in the main theorem statement. Notation V/W/M for three different constants is easy to confuse. Figure 1 is too small to read.
7. [minor] Minimax comparison of two separately worst-cased constants. V_fixed and V_branch are each a sup over populations; the ratio of the two worst cases is a reasonable but somewhat unusual notion of "gap". The paper mitigates this with Theorem 4 and the local-minimax Proposition 22, but the main text should state the caveat once.

**Soundness** 4 — I checked the key proofs (Thms 1–4 and their appendix versions, A20.1, A21) and found them correct; the only issues are loose statements (item 4, 5) rather than errors.

**Presentation** 3 — The main text is precise and well structured, but extremely dense, number-heavy, and leans on a sprawling appendix that includes legacy material; intuition is in short supply.

**Significance** 2 — A natural question answered cleanly, but the identifiable-estimand class is narrow, the worst-case gaps are far from what real models exhibit (1–2×), and the audience (budgeted validation of shallow rules) is niche for ICML.

**Originality** 3 — The model and the exact constants are new and the combination is fresh; the techniques (random-path HT simulation, indexing function, pointer chasing, van Trees/chi-square duality) are all standard.

**Key Questions for Authors**

1. Theorem 4 bounds the second-moment ratio M, while Tables 2–3 report variance ratios W. Please state exactly which quantity "every run lies inside the bracket" refers to, and give the M-ratios alongside the W-ratios. If the W-ratios actually violate N_P in some runs this would be an overclaim; if everything is consistent, this is a clarity fix only.
2. For the pointer-chasing contrast, is V_r(N_{r,κ}) = D exactly (i.e., is the factor b in the lower bound an artifact of the union bound)? More importantly, do you have any example or conjecture about the true two-round exponent: is sup V_2/V_branch of order 2^{(b−1)/2} or 2^{b−2}? A tightened hierarchy (even for r = 2) would materially raise my assessment of the theoretical contribution.
3. How large is the class of deployed models for which the framework actually applies (deg(d) ≤ b)? For thresholded forests, GBDTs or MLPs, can you give a bias–variance tradeoff with a matching lower bound in the approximately-identifiable regime, rather than the one-sided bound (15)? A positive answer would substantially broaden the significance.
4. Relative to Acharya–Canonne–Tyagi / Barnes–Han–Özgür (chi-square contraction/Fisher-information bounds over a channel family), what in Theorem 1 is genuinely new beyond specializing the channel family to coordinate-query trees and allowing label-dependent choice? Please be explicit, since the paper currently credits the result to "established tools" but lists it as the first contribution.
5. How does the exact column generation scale (b = 4 or 5 with m ≈ 12–20)? Is the DP pricing still tractable, and does the 1% uniform smoothing of the held-out law materially affect the reported ratios (e.g., at 0.1% or 5%)? Robustness here affects how much weight the "exact" empirical ratios can bear.

**Limitations**

Yes, largely. Section 8 and the appendices candidly list the assumptions (answers independent of acquisition mode and order, no selective nonresponse, labels observed for every record, finite domains, degree-≤ b contrasts), quantify order effects (A24), certify identification floors for nonlinear models (A26), and discuss cost regimes where complete records beat any capped design (A31). Two suggestions: (i) state in the main text that the pointer-chasing instance has dimension exponential in r and that Corollary 12 is vacuous for b ≤ 6; (ii) say plainly in the abstract that the exponential worst case is not approached by the trained models studied (median 1.5×).

**Overall Recommendation** 3 — Weak reject. A correct, complete and honest treatment of a natural question, but the theoretical results are the ones an expert would anticipate using standard tools, the applicable estimand class is narrow, and the realized gains on real models are modest; for an ICML main-track slot the weaknesses slightly outweigh the merits.

**Confidence** 4 — I checked the central proofs in detail and know the adjacent literature well; I did not verify every one of the 32 appendix sections or the numerical certificates.

**What would move you up one point**

- A tighter round hierarchy: either an exponential lower bound for V_2/V_branch with exponent close to b − 2, or a matching upper bound showing the (b−1)/2 exponent is right (and removing the factor b in D/b).
- A rate-matching theory for the approximately identifiable regime (thresholded forests, GBDT/MLP updates), which would make "accuracy of a frozen classifier" true in the generality the abstract suggests.
- Clear evidence of practical relevance: a realistic setting (larger b, real screening or triage tree) where the exact gain is substantially above 2× and where the bracket of Theorem 4 demonstrably changes a design decision.
- Fixing the M-vs-W bracket statement and tightening the presentation (intuition in the main text, abstract with fewer numbers, appendix trimmed of legacy experiments) would not alone change the score but is needed for any accept.
