\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}
We compute the constants of Sections~\ref{sec:general}--\ref{sec:population} exactly for trained models. The population $P$ is the held-out empirical law mixed with 1\% uniform mass over profiles and labels. It has full support, so $W_\A(P)$ is a local minimax risk. Fixed designs range over all $\binom mb$ forms with the pointwise-unbiased score program of Appendix~\ref{sec:pathdesign}. We report $W_{\rm fixed}/W_{\rm branch}$, the factor by which the best pre-committed design needs more records. \emph{Fitted} designs are optimized on separate reference records and evaluated on $P$.

\paragraph{Independently retrained forests.}
On WDBC, Spambase and Ionosphere \citep{wolberg1993,spambase1999,ionosphere1989}, twenty splits each fit an old and a new forest of twenty depth-3 trees on disjoint training records, using 3, 6, 9 or 12 median-binarized features, with cap $b=3$. The 180 tasks with more than three candidate features are binding: no 3-query tree computes the contrast. Column generation over all depth-3 trees converged on every task, with certified lower and upper bounds equal to relative error $10^{-6}$ (median 50 iterations).

\input{v12_forest_table}

Table~\ref{tab:forestexact} and Figure~\ref{fig:v12main}(a) give three findings. First, adaptivity is worth a median factor 1.51 over the 180 binding tasks (maximum 1.85), and the factor grows with the number of candidate features. Second, a branching menu built from the forests' own component trees captures a median of only 17\% of this gain on the log scale; the optimal designs mix many trees that neither forest contains. Third, even optimal branching stays well above the full-information variance, because averaging forty trees spreads the contrast over more coordinates than any three-question path can read. On the 15 tasks with $m=12$ that we refit, designs optimized on training records realize a median ratio of 1.67 on held-out records against 1.64 for the exact oracles, and a second implementation reproduces every exact ratio to three decimals.

\paragraph{Budgeted screening questionnaires.}
A depth-$b$ screening tree asks at most $b$ questions per person, so a validation study under the same burden must also respect the cap. We train class-balanced CART trees of depth $b\in\{3,4,5,6\}$ on two datasets. The CDC Diabetes Health Indicators data, derived from the Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System \citep{cdcdiabetes2017}, provide twelve yes/no survey items for 253,680 respondents and the target diabetes or prediabetes. The thyroid data provide twelve binarized history and laboratory items for 7,200 patients and an abnormal-function target \citep{quinlan1986thyroid}, a standard benchmark for cost-sensitive test ordering \citep{turney1995}. Trees are trained on source records and validated on a target: a disjoint random half, or respondents of the other sex. The estimand is the target accuracy of the tree's deterministic screening decision. Branching asks the tree's own questions and attains the full-information variance.

Table~\ref{tab:screening} and Figure~\ref{fig:v12main}(b) show that the gain depends on how many questions the screening decision needs. On BRFSS the best pre-committed design needs a median of 1.35--1.58 times as many respondents for $b=4,5,6$, and up to 1.98 times in a single run. On thyroid, every thresholded tree, up to depth six, depends on at most $b$ items. Then $\Gamma_P=1$, the best fixed form computes the decision, and pre-commitment costs nothing intrinsic. The bracket detects this from reference data before any collection. Naive path guessing needs up to 25 times as many respondents, so optimized fixed-form scores remove most of the cost of pre-commitment and leave only the intrinsic part. That part accrues over rounds, as Theorem~\ref{thm:rounds} predicts. Asking the root question first recovers a median 33--63\% of the log-gap, and a third round recovers 57--100\%. Fitted designs lose 3--7\% relative to their oracles. Every run lies inside the bracket of Theorem~\ref{thm:bracket}, and its lower end $1/\Gamma_P$ is the closer bound.

Multiple imputation from complete source records is a model-based alternative. In simulated audits of 1,000 respondents, its RMSE is 0.88--1.00 times that of branching, with coverage 0.940--0.993, under both targets (Appendix~\ref{app:screening}). Here the conditional law of unasked items given asked items transports, and imputation imports information from complete source records that the audit itself does not collect. The design-based scores need neither the reference nor the transport assumption, and their RMSE is 1.00--1.22 times that of branching.

\paragraph{A real planned-missing archive.}
PISA 2012 assigned three questionnaire forms at random, and no U.S. student answered all nine predictors of our block-averaged forest ensembles. Every block pair appears on a form, so the Brier and accuracy contrasts are identifiable from the actual menu. Optimized form scores, a block/pair Horvitz--Thompson score and multiple imputation give similar estimates, and all Brier intervals include zero (Appendix~\ref{sec:exp}). This archive demonstrates identification and survey inference from real fixed forms; it has no branching counterfactual.
