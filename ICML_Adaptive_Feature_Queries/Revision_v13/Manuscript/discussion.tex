\section{Related Work}
Fixed forms implement planned missingness and matrix sampling
\citep{raghunathan1995,graham2006,adiguzel2008,chipperfield2018,ncesbooklets}.
Branching is a designed coarsening mechanism using observed answers
\citep{heitjan1991,gill1997}. Known-coarsening efficiency, augmentation
and optimized two-phase sampling have an extensive theory
\citep{robins1994,tsiatis2006,hahn2011,tao2020,berrett2024}.
Our population-specific local result applies this theory to the finite
interview menu. Proposition~\ref{prop:pointwise}
adds a finite-$n$ statement for globally unbiased, possibly nonlinear
sequential estimators, including boundary populations. The complete-form
program implements the known influence-function constraint in a sparse
product basis. Responsive survey designs use accumulating paradata to change
collection effort and control nonresponse and costs \citep{groves2006}.
Our distinction is feedback within a record under a fixed query cap.
Multistage and adaptive tests usually target individual
ability rather than a population model contrast \citep{vanderlinden2010,yan2014}.

The minimax tools draw on convex duality, moduli and optimal contrast design
\citep{donoho1991,donoho1994,juditsky2009,polyanskiy2026,elfving1952,sagnol2011}.
The information-contraction argument has direct precedents in inference
under restricted channels and communication \citep{acharya2019,barnes2020,acharya2022}.
Classical adaptivity results depend on the information model
\citep{novak1996,arias2013,gupta2016}; huge-object testing explicitly
separates queries within and across samples \citep{goldreich2022,chakraborty2023,adar2024}.
Reading every node of a depth-$b$ binary tree uses at most $2^b-1$ fixed
queries. That classical simulation violates our per-person cap. Randomly
selecting a path preserves the cap and pays through score variance instead.
Address examples in stochastic Boolean evaluation compare query cost on
one input \citep{hellerstein2022}; here the loss is respondent count for
estimating a population functional. Local differential privacy also separates
forms of interaction \citep{joseph2019}, but constrains randomized privacy
channels rather than the number of revealed coordinates. Our contribution is
the exact coefficient under the stated query constraints, including
cross-block allocations and mixed expected/maximum budgets.

Active evaluation and prediction-powered inference allocate labels or
combine proxy predictions with complete corrections
\citep{sawade2010,sawade2012,kossen2021,angelopoulos2023,zrnic2024,ppi2024}.
Feature-value acquisition can support model induction \citep{saartsechansky2009}
or individual prediction \citep{ma2019,covert2023,trapeznikov2013,nan2017,vonkleist2025}.
EDDI selects information for target-variable prediction; conditional-mutual-information
policies similarly optimize individual predictive loss. Restricted-attribute learning
fits a predictor \citep{bendavid1998,birkendorf1998,cesa2011}.
MultiPPI jointly allocates observations and estimator weights under an overall
budget, with a minimax characterization for known covariance classes
\citep{cowenbreen2026}. Active MultiPPI studies instance-conditional predictor
routing and label allocation under expected cost \citep{brawand2026}.
Our sharp comparison instead fixes the maximum unknown coordinates revealed
from each record and allows feedback within that record.
Our target is a frozen population contrast, with input acquisition charged
per person. A proxy-plus-complete-residual design becomes a natural
alternative when some people may exceed the feature cap.

\section{Choosing a Collection Design}\label{sec:guidance}

comparison. A historical sample can estimate the target directly when it
represents the target population; an external reference can calibrate scores
without that transport assumption. Table~\ref{tab:decision} gives defaults that do not require
an unobserved validation estimate of imputation bias.

Fixed-form scores are the default when the acquisition protocol must stay
fixed, the cap is binding, and inference must remain valid without trusting
an imputation model. These conditions define their use, rather than a claim
that they dominate branching or admissible complete-record collection.
When reference records describe another population, their direct mean is a
transport estimator and needs its own justification. Pointwise-unbiased form
scores instead target the population supplying the new audit records.
With no calibration, a uniform mixture over path sets and completion
weighting is a simple valid default when a capped tree computes the contrast.
A conservative reference can instead fit prefix or form scores; a wrong
reference affects variance, not unbiasedness. A restricted pilot can improve
the reference, but its acquisition cost and reuse variance must be included.
The experiments report multiple pilot sizes and weights rather than select
a universal pilot fraction from evaluation data.

For frozen imputation bias $\mathfrak b_I$ and variances $\sigma_I^2,\sigma_U^2$,
imputation has smaller MSE exactly when
$n\mathfrak b_I^2<\sigma_U^2-\sigma_I^2$. This explains why a fitted proxy can win at
small $n$ and lose as the audit grows with calibration held fixed.
For inference about $\Delta$, use a certified bias bound or independent
validation. A proxy's uncorrected interval covers its own mean. The
bias-expanded interval in Section~2 instead carries the approximation error
explicitly; it remains informative only when that error is below the desired
precision. Its finite-domain implementation and continuous-model rejection
certificates are in Appendix~\ref{app:workflow}.

The sharp gap concerns a hard cap and a supremum over dimensions.
The fixed dimension-to-cap frontier and an exponential two-round versus
fully sequential separation remain undetermined. The independent-forest
experiment gives modest gains for the tested branching menu, while
controlled tree refinements give larger certified population gaps. PISA
supplies an actual secondary model comparison on incomplete archival data,
with no observed complete-data truth or branching alternative. The
GBDT/MLP experiments instead test budget feasibility. These analyses do
not establish deployment performance or a typical exponential benefit.

Order and context can change measured answers \citep{schuman1981}.
Appendix~\ref{app:measurement} explicitly distinguishes the latent target
from the mean score under a collection-specific measurement channel.
A prospective bridge design should randomize collection mode, randomize
permitted item positions, and retain common anchors at matched positions.
Within each prespecified subgroup, report response rates, query counts and
anchor differences by mode. These comparisons can detect measurement and
burden disparities; they do not identify every unobserved counterfactual
answer. The present data provide retrospective and sensitivity evidence.
