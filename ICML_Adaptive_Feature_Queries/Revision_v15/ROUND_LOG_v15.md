# v15 revision loop — round log (continues ROUND_LOG_v14.md)

## Round 5 — novelty gate (web search, 2026-11-11)
Question: after the round-4 reviews, is the direction pre-empted or reduced to a narrow remainder? Two of the three round-4 reviewers read the population-level program as classical c-optimal design and the converse of Theorem 1 as a known channel-family argument, so this gate focused on experimental design, two-phase sampling, design-based minimax theory and recent (2025-2026) work on the value of interaction. arxiv.org is not reachable from the sandbox; abstracts were read through search results and secondary pages.

Searches and what they returned:
- Optimal split-questionnaire / planned-missing designs: Chipperfield & Steel; Imbriano & Raghunathan 2020; Jang, Zhu & Yu (arXiv 2203.04883, A-optimal split questionnaire designs); Bradlow-type KL designs. All optimize a distribution over fixed forms under a working model or a design criterion. None compares fixed forms with respondent-level adaptive interviews, and none imposes a per-respondent cap with design-unbiased estimation.
- Two-phase designs: Gilbert, Yu & Rotnitzky 2014 (Stat. Med. 33:901-917; optimal phase-two probabilities for the semiparametric efficient estimator of a mean); Reilly & Pepe mean score; McIsaac & Cook 2015 (adaptive two-phase with an internal pilot); Tao, Zeng & Lin 2020 (JASA 115:1946-1959); Wang, Wang & Miao (arXiv 2312.10596, maximin designs). These are within-record adaptive designs with one binary decision (measure the expensive block or not) under an expected budget. They do not treat a hard cap on which of m items are revealed, round hierarchies, or class-comparison constants. They are the right lineage for the population program, the label-dependent designs and Proposition 6, and v15 says so.
- Design-based minimax: Stenger 1979 (Ann. Statist. 7:395-399); Gabler 1990 (LNS 64); Aronow & Lopatto 2026 (arXiv 2605.20572, minimax design-unbiased estimation with bounded outcomes; midpoint-differenced Horvitz-Thompson). Full observation of the sampled units; no partial feature views.
- Information-based complexity: Bakhvalov 1971; Traub-Wasilkowski-Wozniakowski; Novak 1996 (J. Complexity 12:199-237): adaption does not help for linear functionals on convex balanced classes. This is the classical analogue of our across-record statement; within-record adaptivity under a cap is a nonlinear information restriction and is not covered.
- Value of interaction, 2025-2026: Xu 2026 (arXiv 2608.06262, hypothesis testing with conditional queries: a worst-case N^2 adaptivity gap in a conditional-sampling oracle model; no per-record feature cap, no estimation constants); adaptive sensing for principal components (arXiv 2505.10882, a factor-d gap for linear measurements); Lau & Scarlett (1-bit mean estimation; already cited); adaptive experimental design with semiparametric efficiency (Zhang & van der Laan 2026; treatment assignment). None studies feature queries under a per-record cap.
- Active feature acquisition evaluation (von Kleist et al.; already cited): estimates the value of a given acquisition policy from retrospective data, not the statistical cost of the acquisition class.

Verdict: PASS, with a narrower claim of novelty than v14 made. No prior or concurrent work was found that gives (i) the sharp records ratio between pre-committed, r-round and branching feature queries under a hard per-record cap, (ii) a round hierarchy on the variance scale, or (iii) exact pricing of r-round interviews in a design program. What is classical, and is now credited as such in the paper: the population program for a fixed menu is c-optimal design with an Elfving-type dual; its fixed-design score is the efficient augmented inverse-probability estimator under known coarsening; column generation is a vertex-direction method; across-record adaptivity not helping is the statistical analogue of the adaption results of information-based complexity; two-phase designs are two-round interviews under an expected budget. The remaining contribution is a comparison of protocol classes, which is not a narrow remainder of an existing result: no cited or found paper answers it even in a special case. The practical scope (one known low-degree contrast, small caps, labels observed) is a limitation of the setting, not a pre-emption, and is stated in the paper.

References named by the simulated round-4 reviewers and checked in this gate: Gilbert-Yu-Rotnitzky 2014 (confirmed), Stenger 1979 (confirmed), Gabler 1990 (confirmed), Bakhvalov 1971 and Novak 1996 (confirmed), Tao-Zeng-Lin 2020 (confirmed), McIsaac & Cook 2015 (confirmed through secondary citations; Stat. Med., doi 10.1002/sim.6523). "Smolyak" could not be confirmed as a source for the adaption result and is not cited.

## Round 5 — experiment results at the v15 freeze
- Label-dependent fixed designs (30 screening runs at b <= 5): the records ratio falls by at most 0.5%; with sex strata (9 runs) by at most 0.8%.
- Balanced random forms (uniform over all C(12,b) forms), BRFSS b = 4-6: 9-23 times the records of branching with optimal scores, 23-52 times with monomial Horvitz-Thompson scores; optimized forms 1.00-1.98.
- Forest updates, exact two-round designs (60 tasks): W_2/W_branch median 1.04, at most 1.09; recovered share of the log-gap 80-95%, median 89%; three rounds at b = 4 recover a median 98%. Cap versus pre-commitment: fixed/full medians 1.4-4.1, branching/full 1.1-3.3 by cap.
- Two-round computability: 17 of 24 BRFSS trees and all three 21-item trees at b = 4 are computed exactly by a two-round interview; the enumeration agrees with the solver on 43 of 43 runs. Not computable: 2 of 6 at b = 5, 5 of 6 at b = 6.
- BRFSS two-round designs: exact at b <= 5 (at most 1.040); b = 6: S0 1.000, S1 1.013 (closed), R2 [1.012, 1.031], R1 <= 1.063, S2 <= 1.109, R0 <= 1.320 (60 minutes of column generation each). Fitted two-round designs at b <= 5: median 1.01, at most 1.05.
- Pilot-learned path-set designs (40 pilots per run and size): excess variance over the oracle path-set design, median over runs: b = 4: 17%, 9%, 5%, 3%, 2% at n1 = 250, 500, 1000, 2000, 4000; b = 5: 86%, 38%, 19%, 10%, 5%; b = 6: far from the oracle below n1 = 4000 (about 30% at 4000). Reference-fitted designs: 1-3%.
- Log-linear working model on the capped records (400 replications): at n = 1000 RMSE 0.77-0.97 times the design-based form score and 0.90-1.01 times branching, coverage 0.920-0.968; at n = 16,000 coverage 0.855-0.953 (below 0.90 in 4 of 10 settings) and RMSE above the form score in 5 of 10 settings (all five b = 4 settings worse or equal, four of five b = 5 settings still better). On balanced random forms the same model has RMSE 0.99-1.78 times the optimized forms' design-based score at n = 1000.
- 21-item study: the three trees at b = 4 coincide with the twelve-item trees of the same seeds; the study tests program size only.
- Incident: the exact b = 6 two-round job was stopped by the two-hour background limit after ten concurrent jobs oversubscribed the four cores (see HANDOFF_v15.md).
