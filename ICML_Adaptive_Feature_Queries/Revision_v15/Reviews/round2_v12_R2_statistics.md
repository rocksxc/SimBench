**Summary**

The paper studies how many more records a data collector needs when it must pre-commit to which b of m binary features to observe per record ("fixed forms", i.e., split-questionnaire / matrix-sampling designs) versus choosing each feature after seeing earlier answers ("branching", i.e., a depth-b decision-tree interview), when the estimand is a known linear functional of the record, Δ(P) = E_P[Y d(X)], such as the accuracy of a frozen rule or the accuracy difference between two frozen rules. Labels are always observed; only features are capped. The main results are: (i) for any finite menu of interviews, the first-order minimax MSE constant is a finite convex program with a dual "signal / best single-interview projection energy" form, with a converse (via change of measure, KL chain rule and van Trees) that holds against biased estimators, across-record adaptation and label-dependent interview choice; (ii) a sharp worst-case ratio V_fixed/V_branch ≤ min{2^{b−1}, C(m−1,b−1)}, attained by an "address/indexing" contrast under the uniform law; (iii) a rounds hierarchy: r batches lose at most 2^{b−r}, and pointer-chasing contrasts separate r from r+1 rounds by 2^{⌊(b−1)/r⌋}/b; (iv) a population-specific bracket 1/Γ_P ≤ M_fixed/M_branch ≤ N_P(τ) where Γ_P is the energy fraction explained by the best single form and N_P is the Rényi-1/2 effective number of path sets of the tree; (v) a column-generation algorithm with exact DP pricing that computes the branching constant over all depth-b trees with certified bounds, and an SOCP over Fourier coefficients for the optimal fixed-form design jointly with its optimal (AIPW-type) augmentation. Experiments on depth-3 random-forest "updates" (WDBC, Spambase, Ionosphere; 180 binding tasks) give a median exact ratio 1.51; on BRFSS screening trees with b = 4–6 the ratio is 1.35–1.58 (max 1.98), on thyroid trees it is exactly 1 because the trees use ≤ b distinct items; a completion-only "path guessing" baseline needs up to 25× more records; MI from complete source records matches or beats branching; a PISA 2012 secondary analysis illustrates identification and Fay-BRR inference from real rotated forms.

**Strengths**

1. The question is well-posed and the worst-case answer is complete and clean. The reduction "simulate a tree by drawing one of its ≤ 2^{b−1} padded path sets and Horvitz–Thompson-rescale on completion" and the matching address-function lower bound (energy r·2^{s−2κ} ≤ 2^{−κ}) are correct and elegant. I checked the dual derivation (Sion + Fenchel), the Fisher-information/van Trees converse, the pointer-chasing round-elimination argument, and the Cauchy–Schwarz/Rényi bracket; I found no errors.
2. The lower bounds are genuinely stronger than classical efficiency theory: they are worst-case over populations, hold for biased and sequentially adaptive procedures (including label-first interview choice), and the local-asymptotic-minimax counterpart (Prop. 22) is stated with the correct full-support caveat. The paper is explicit that the fixed-form program is the known-coarsening influence-function bound of Robins–Rotnitzky–Zhao/Tsiatis optimized jointly over the coarsening mechanism, not a new efficiency principle. This honesty is welcome.
3. The computational contribution is real: exact column generation over ~1.5×10^7 depth-3 trees with DP pricing in O(m^b 2^b) and certified primal/dual gaps of 10^{−6}, and a fixed-form SOCP in Fourier coordinates with O(C(m,b)2^b) variables that never enumerates 2^m profiles, whose unbiasedness is invariant to misspecification of the reference marginals (only variance is affected). The finding that menus restricted to the forests' own trees recover only ~17% of the log-gain is a non-obvious and useful empirical fact.
4. The population-specific bracket is interpretable and actually predictive in the experiments (thyroid: Γ_P = 1 ⇒ no gain, detected before any collection).
5. The evaluation is unusually careful statistically: oracle vs. fitted designs, a mean-vs-hard cap analysis, a rounds analysis, proper MI with Bayesian-bootstrap hot deck and Rubin's rules with coverage reported, order-effect sensitivity (A24), identification-floor certificates for GBDT/MLP (A26), and a PISA analysis that uses the correct Fay BRR (k = 0.5) domain-estimation formula and the correct QUESTID form identifier. Limitations are stated plainly throughout.

**Weaknesses**

1. [major] Narrow practical scope relative to the framing. The theory requires a known, frozen, low-degree contrast on a finite (binary/ordinal) cube with labels always observed. The abstract and introduction frame the problem as general survey/clinical data collection, but for the estimands survey methodologists actually plan matrix-sampling designs around (item means, pairwise associations, regression coefficients), the contrast has degree ≤ 2 and a single form computes it, so adaptivity has zero value by the paper's own Theorem 4. The gain exists only for tree-structured, high-degree targets, and the paper's own Figure 4/A26 shows that ordinary GBDT (logistic link) and MLP updates on 12 binarized features are not identifiable until ~8–12 queries, i.e., essentially the full record. The realized gains for the admissible class (depth-3 forests of 20 trees fit on 60–500 records; CART screening trees) are 1–2×, and zero on one of two screening datasets.
2. [major] The headline "naive pre-committed designs need up to 25 times as many respondents" is against a straw man (uniform path guessing with completion-only scores, i.e., the TCS simulation that discards every observed answer). No statistician would use it; AIPW or MI is the natural baseline, and against those the cost of pre-commitment is 1.0–1.6×. The abstract should state this comparison as what it is.
3. [major] The "exact" population ratios are computed at an artificial law: the held-out empirical distribution (as few as 171 Ionosphere or 269 WDBC records on a 4,096-cell cube) mixed with 1% uniform mass. Both programs can tailor scores to the ~200 occupied cells while unbiasedness on the remaining ~3,900 cells costs ≈10^{−6} each; the richer branching menu has more freedom to do so. The fitted-design check (1.67 vs. 1.64 at m = 12) partly mitigates this, but no sensitivity to the smoothing mass (0.1%, 1%, 5%, 10%) or to evaluation-sample size is reported (A23.3 varies only the design-probability floor β on different, legacy tasks), and the choices 1%/10% are unexplained.
4. [major] Presentation. The main text is extremely dense, juggles V, W, M, Φ_n, Γ_P, N_P, K_{m,b}, L_τ for closely related constants, and relies on a 48-page appendix that is a revision archive rather than a supplement: A8 "retains the experiments of the previous version of the study" on SUSHI/SAPA datasets never mentioned in the main body, refers to "PROTOCOL v10.json", "the earlier release", "1,048 retained release checks", and a four-feature counterexample with an irrational optimum. Figure 1 is nearly illegible at print size. A reader cannot verify the main claims without wading through all of this; much of A8, A13–A17, A19–A23, A25, A28, A30.3 and A31 should be cut or moved to a separate technical report.
5. [minor] Theorem 4 brackets the second-moment ratio M_fixed/M_branch, while Table 2 reports and checks the variance ratio ("records needed"), which is larger whenever Δ ≠ 0 (W-ratio = (M_f − Δ²)/(M_b − Δ²); with Δ ≈ 0.13–0.35 and E t² = 1/4 here, the difference is not negligible). The lower bound still transfers, but the statement "every run lies inside the bracket" should be made precise, and the main text should state the conversion ρ_P given only in A4.
6. [minor] The sentence "Ratios W_fixed(P)/W_branch(P) therefore compare protocol classes for every estimator, including imputation-based ones" (Sec. 3) overclaims: it holds for regular estimators using the audit data alone in a local sense; MI that borrows complete source records is outside this information set, as Section 6 then shows by MI beating branching. Also, a fairer design-based comparator to MI would be a model-assisted fixed-form estimator that uses the same source records (e.g., HT + source-fitted E[t | X_asked, Y] augmentation), which would isolate the design effect from the information effect.
7. [minor] The PISA analysis is statistically competent but, as the authors concede, "has no branching counterfactual" and demonstrates only that a pairwise-covered Brier contrast is identifiable from three rotated forms; all intervals include zero. Listing it among the contributions and in the abstract inflates its role.
8. [minor] Related work misses literature a survey/psychometrics reader would expect: multistage adaptive testing in large-scale assessments (PISA 2018 reading MST; NAEP MST designs), where "stages" are exactly the paper's rounds and the goal is population-level reporting; computerized adaptive testing generally; optimal split-questionnaire design (e.g., Adigüzel & Wedel 2008 JMR; Thomas et al. 2006; Rhemtulla & Little 2012); and optimal multiwave two-phase sampling (Chen & Lumley 2020/2022) beyond Tao et al.
9. [minor] A24 shows that at a 10% last-position answer-flip probability the measurement bias is 0.5–1.8 branching SEs at n = 1000, i.e., of the same order as the entire 1.3–1.6× sampling gain. This is the first-order practical issue for branching questionnaires in surveys and deserves more than one sentence in the main text.
10. [minor] Experimental realism: forests are fit on 60–500 records with median-binarized features; the "update" (old vs. new forest) is artificial; 20 overlapping splits are reported with percentiles that are not confidence intervals (acknowledged). The screening-tree gains are not monotone in b (1.49, 1.35, 1.58), and only three seeds × two targets are used.

**Soundness** (3): The theorems are correct where I checked them and the computations are certified, but the exact ratios depend on an unexamined smoothing of very sparse empirical laws, and the main-text bracket check conflates second-moment and variance ratios.

**Presentation** (2): The main body is dense but readable with effort; the 48-page appendix is a revision archive with legacy experiments and datasets absent from the main text, and notation is heavy.

**Significance** (3): A complete, memorable worst-case answer and a useful exact computational tool for budgeted evaluation, but the admissible class of targets is narrow and realized gains are 1–2× (often 1) and comparable to plausible order effects.

**Originality** (3): The ingredients (HT path simulation, indexing/pointer-chasing lower bounds, Le Cam/van Trees, AIPW efficiency under known coarsening) are known; their combination into exact constants comparing acquisition classes, the rounds hierarchy, and the certified column generation over all trees are new.

**Key Questions for Authors**

1. Recompute Table 1 (and Table 2 "Best") with uniform smoothing masses of 0.1%, 1%, 5% and 10%, and with bootstrap resamples of the held-out set. If the exact ratios move by less than a few percent, Weakness 3 disappears and I would raise Soundness to 4; if they move substantially, the "exact" ratios are properties of the smoothing, not of the data.
2. Which realistic model classes satisfy deg(d) ≤ b at caps of 3–6 besides depth-limited probability-averaging forests? Can you give one example of a deployed-scale update (e.g., additive/identity-link GBDT, or a depth-b tree distilled from a larger model with a certified floor) where the ratio is computed? A credible non-toy example would raise Significance.
3. For the MI comparison, what does a model-assisted fixed-form estimator that uses the same source records (HT plus source-fitted conditional mean augmentation) achieve? If it matches MI, the paper's message becomes "information, not design, closes the gap", which is cleaner and affects how I read Section 6.
4. Does any screening run have variance ratio above N_P, and can you state Theorem 4 (or Table 2) consistently in one of the two scales? This is a precision issue; a clear answer would remove Weakness 5.
5. How do your rounds results relate to multistage adaptive testing as used in PISA 2018 and NAEP, where population-level precision is the design goal? If the MST literature already contains analogous stage-vs-fixed efficiency comparisons, Originality would be somewhat lower; if not, saying so explicitly strengthens the paper.

**Limitations**

Mostly yes: Section 8, the Impact Statement, A24 (order effects) and A26 (identification floors) are candid. I would add explicitly in the main text that (a) the framework covers one known low-degree functional per design and does not address multipurpose surveys, where matrix sampling is actually used; (b) the measurement-invariance assumption between fixed and branching protocols is the first-order practical obstacle and can erase the sampling gain (A24); and (c) "exact" population constants are at a smoothed empirical law whose smoothing has not been varied. The abstract should also label the 25× figure as a completion-only path-guessing baseline.

**Overall Recommendation**: 4 — Weak accept. Technically solid and honest, with a sharp worst-case theorem, a correct extension of known-coarsening efficiency theory to adaptive/biased procedures, and a genuinely useful exact computation; held back by a narrow admissible class of targets, modest realized gains, an inflated headline baseline, an unexamined smoothing choice behind the "exact" ratios, and a sprawling appendix that materially hurts verifiability.

**Confidence**: 4. I checked the key proofs (Theorems 1–4, the address and pointer-chasing constructions, Prop. 22), the two score programs, the MI and PISA details, and the forest grid; I am less able to judge how the TCS community would weigh the round-hierarchy result relative to existing property-testing/communication results.

**What would move you up one point**

- A sensitivity table for the smoothing mass and evaluation-sample size behind Table 1/Table 2 showing the exact ratios are stable (addresses Weakness 3).
- At least one non-toy identifiable target at a meaningful cap (e.g., a distilled or additive model with a certified floor) with a computed ratio clearly above 1.5 and above the A24 measurement-effect noise floor, or a demonstration that the framework handles several functionals jointly.
- A main text that reports the straw-man 25× honestly, states the bracket and the reported ratios in one scale, and a supplement trimmed to what the paper uses (remove the "earlier version" experiments or move them to a technical report), with consistent notation.
- Related-work coverage of multistage adaptive testing/CAT and optimal split-questionnaire and multiwave two-phase design, positioning the rounds result against them.
