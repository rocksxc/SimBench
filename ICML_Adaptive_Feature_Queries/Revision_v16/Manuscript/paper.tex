\begin{abstract}
Rotated questionnaire booklets, laboratory panels and budgeted classifiers reveal at most $b$ of $m$ features per record. When each record's questions are fixed in advance instead of chosen from its earlier answers, how many more records does a design-unbiased estimate of a known low-degree functional, such as the accuracy of a frozen screening rule, need? For trained screening trees and forest updates the answer is modest: optimized pre-committed forms need between $1.0$ and \nMaxAll{} times the records of branching interviews, and one adaptive round recovers most of the gap: at least \nTwoMinPct\% wherever the optimal two-round design was computed, and at least \nTwoBoundRec\% by certified bounds elsewhere. The worst case is exponentially larger, and we characterize it exactly. At every population, forms need at most $2^{b-1}$ times the records of branching, with equality at populations of any accuracy. With $r$ rounds of questions the factor is at most $2^{b-r}$, yet there are targets on at most $b^2$ features for which a constant number of rounds still loses $2^{b(1-o(1))}$, so a protocol within a polynomial factor of branching on every target needs a number of rounds linear in $b$. The constants are computable: the minimax constant of a protocol class is a convex program, and a column-generation solver with dynamic-programming pricing certifies optimal fixed, $r$-round and branching designs at a population. Design-unbiasedness has its own price: \nAbstractModel
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Population quantities are often estimated from records that each reveal only a few features. Questionnaires cap respondent burden, clinical protocols cap the tests per patient, and budgeted classifiers acquire a few inputs per case \citep{turney1995,trapeznikov2013,nan2017}. A collector can pre-commit to each record's feature set, as in split questionnaires, rotated booklets and laboratory panels ordered at once \citep{raghunathan1995,chipperfield2009}, or it can choose each question after seeing earlier answers, as in computer-assisted skip logic, adaptive matrix sampling \citep{gonzalez2008} and reflex testing, where a second assay is run only if the first is abnormal. This paper asks how many more records pre-commitment costs, and how much of the loss a few rounds of questions recover, when the target is a population quantity such as the accuracy of a frozen screening rule or the accuracy gain of a model update.

We study the question in a design-based regime. The label of every sampled record is observed and charged separately, the target is one known contrast of degree at most $b$, answers do not depend on question order, and estimators are unbiased by design, with no model for the unasked features. This is a deliberately demanding benchmark: a parsimonious model fitted to the capped records, or imputation from a complete reference that transports to the target, can be more accurate at moderate sample sizes, and Section~\ref{sec:experiments} measures when.

That adaptivity within one input can be worth exponentially many queries is classical: a depth-$b$ decision tree is simulated non-adaptively only by reading all of its $2^b-1$ nodes, and the huge-object model and interactive local privacy separate protocol classes by query or sample complexity \citep{goldreich2022,adar2024,joseph2019,joseph2020}. Under a hard per-record cap the price is paid in records instead, and the questions become quantitative: what is the exact price, how much of it do a few rounds recover, and how large is it at the population at hand?

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt\parskip0pt
\item \emph{Exact constants (Theorems~\ref{thm:char} and~\ref{thm:universal}).} The first-order minimax constant of any finite menu of interviews is a convex program, and neither adaptation across records nor label-dependent choices change it. Pre-committed forms need at most $K_{m,b}=\min\{2^{b-1},\binom{m-1}{b-1}\}$ times the records of branching at \emph{every} population; an address contrast attains $2^{b-1}$ at populations of every accuracy.
\item \emph{Rounds (Theorem~\ref{thm:rounds}).} Interviews with $r$ batches need at most $2^{b-r}$ times the records of branching. Conversely a binary pointer tree loses $2^{b-1}/(1+2b/r)^r$ in $r$ rounds, and its products do so on at most $b^2$ features, so polynomially competitive protocols need more than $0.37b-O(\log b)$ rounds.
\item \emph{Population value and computation (Theorem~\ref{thm:bracket}).} At a population the ratio lies between $1/\rho_P^2$, where $\rho_P^2$ is the largest share of the target's variance that one form explains, and an effective number of question paths. A column-generation solver with exact dynamic-programming pricing certifies the optimal design of every protocol class without enumerating the $2^m$ profiles.
\item \emph{What the constants are on trained models (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).} Optimized forms cost a factor between $1.0$ and \nMaxAll; a second round recovers \nTwoForestLo--\nTwoForestHi\% of the log-gap for forest updates and at least \nScreenTwoRecMin\% for screening trees at caps up to five; designs learned from reference records or a pilot stay exactly unbiased; and a two-way working model on the same records is more accurate than the design-based score for every tree up to \nCurveLastAllBetter{} records and less accurate for a third of them at 16,000.
\end{itemize}

\section{Estimand and Acquisition Protocols}\label{sec:setup}
Let $X$ take values in $\X=\{-1,1\}^m$ and let $Y\in\{-1,1\}$ be a label. Freeze two rules $p_0,p_1:\X\to[0,1]$ that predict $+1$ with probabilities $p_j(x)$, put $d=p_1-p_0$, $t(x,y)=yd(x)$, and
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(P)=\E_P[t(X,Y)]=\E_P[Yd(X)].\label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
This is the accuracy difference of the two rules; $p_0\equiv1/2$ makes it affine in the accuracy of $p_1$ alone, and Brier improvements are handled in the same way (Appendix~\ref{app:metrics}). Labels are recorded for every sampled record and charged separately; the cap $b$ bounds the feature queries per record and is \emph{binding} when $m>b$.

\paragraph{Protocols.}
An interview $\iota$ maps a profile $x$ to a transcript $\ell_\iota(x)$. A \emph{fixed form} queries a coordinate set chosen before the record is seen. An \emph{$r$-round interview} queries batches $B_1,B_2(x_{B_1}),\ldots,B_r$, each chosen from the answers to earlier batches, with $\sum_s|B_s|\le b$. A \emph{branching interview} is a decision tree of depth at most $b$. The menus satisfy $\A_{\rm fixed}=\A_1\subseteq\A_2\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\A_b=\A_{\rm branch}$. Across records, a protocol may use all previous records to choose the next interview, and our lower bounds even allow the current label to be seen first. $R_\A(d,n)$ is the minimax mean squared error over protocols and estimators that use $n$ records and interviews from $\A$.

\paragraph{Identification.}
Let $\Hh_\A$ be the span of functions constant on the transcript cells of $\A$. The contrast is identifiable for all populations exactly when $d\in\Hh_\A$; for fixed forms of size $b$ and for depth-$b$ trees this is the same condition, Fourier degree $\deg(d)\le b$ in the characters $\chi_S(x)=\prod_{j\in S}x_j$. It holds for every probability average of depth-$b$ trees, such as a depth-limited random forest, and it fails for most thresholded or link-transformed ensembles at small caps; Appendix~\ref{app:G} certifies identification floors for that case.

\paragraph{A worked example.}
Let $m=3$, $b=2$ and $d(x)=[(1+x_1)x_2+(1-x_1)x_3]/2$: the first answer selects which of two others is the target (Figure~\ref{fig:queries}). Branching reads $x_1$ and then the selected coordinate, so it observes $t$ and needs $\Var_P(t)$. Under uniform profiles and an independent uniform label, every form of size two explains exactly half of the variance of $t$, and the tree's two question paths are equally likely. Theorem~\ref{thm:bracket} then gives $W_{\rm fixed}/W_{\rm branch}=2=K_{3,2}$: the best pre-committed design draws $\{1,2\}$ or $\{1,3\}$ with probability $1/2$ and doubles the score when the drawn form contains the selected coordinate, and it needs twice as many records.

\section{An Exact Characterization}\label{sec:general}
Choose interview probabilities $q_\iota$ and transcript tables $g_\iota$. The score $Yg_\iota(\ell_\iota(X))/q_\iota$ is unbiased for $Yd(X)$ at every profile exactly when $\sum_\iota g_\iota(\ell_\iota(x))=d(x)$ for all $x$. Define
\begin{equation}
 V(d,\A)=\inf_{\substack{q,g:\ \sum_\iota g_\iota=d}}\ \max_{x\in\X}
             \sum_\iota\frac{g_\iota(\ell_\iota(x))^2}{q_\iota},\label{eq:unbiasedprogram}
\end{equation}
where a zero-probability interview has a zero table.

\begin{theorem}[Exact characterization]\label{thm:char}
Let $\X$ and $\A$ be finite and $d\in\Hh_\A$. Then $nR_\A(d,n)\to V(d,\A)$, and an iid design with the unbiased score attains $V/n$ for every $n$. The converse applies to biased estimators, to designs that adapt across records, and to interview choices that depend on the current label. Moreover
\[
 V(d,\A)=\sup_{\mu,h}\frac{\langle d,h\rangle_\mu^2}{\max_{\iota\in\A}\|\E_\mu[h\mid\ell_\iota(X)]\|_\mu^2},
\]
over strictly positive profile laws $\mu$ and witnesses $h$ with nonzero denominator.
\end{theorem}
The dual compares the signal $\langle d,h\rangle_\mu$ of a perturbation of the label mean with the largest projection energy that one interview can capture; a change of measure, the KL chain rule and a local van Trees argument turn this into minimax risk (Appendix~\ref{app:B}, with a finite-sample version). The ingredients are standard: minimax duality for linear functionals \citep{donoho1994,juditsky2009,polyanskiy2026} and information bounds that hold simultaneously over a family of channels, including sequentially chosen ones \citep{acharya2019,barnes2020}. We use them to obtain \emph{exact} constants for each query class, which is what a comparison of classes requires. That adaptation across records does not change the constant is the statistical counterpart of the classical fact that adaption does not help for linear problems \citep{bakhvalov1971,novak1996}; adaptivity \emph{within} a record changes the information operator itself.

\paragraph{Population-specific constants.}
For a fixed law $P$ of $(X,Y)$, let
\begin{equation}
 W_{\A}(P)=\inf_{q,H:\,\E[H\mid X,Y]=t(X,Y)}\Var_{P,q}(H),\label{eq:mainW}
\end{equation}
where the score $H$ of the drawn interview's transcript and the label must be unbiased at every profile and label but may be tuned to $P$. At a full-support $P$, $W_\A(P)/n$ is the local asymptotic minimax risk over arbitrary estimators that use the capped records alone (Proposition~\ref{prop:local}), so $W_{\rm fixed}(P)/W_{\rm branch}(P)$ is the ratio of records the two classes need near $P$ when $n$ is large relative to the number of cells and each class uses its best design for $P$; Section~\ref{sec:experiments} examines learned designs and moderate $n$. For a fixed menu, \eqref{eq:mainW} is a $c$-optimal design problem whose design points are interviews, with an Elfving-type dual, and for a fixed design its score is the efficient augmented inverse-probability estimator under known coarsening \citep{elfving1952,pukelsheim1993,robins1994,tsiatis2006}. What is new here is the comparison \emph{across} menus and the exact optimization over the combinatorial menus of $r$-round and branching interviews.

\section{How Much Can Adaptivity Be Worth?}\label{sec:gap}
\begin{theorem}[The gap and its extremes]\label{thm:universal}
Let $1\le b\le m$ and let $d$ be a nonzero contrast of degree at most $b$ on $\{-1,1\}^m$, with $K_{m,b}=\min\{2^{b-1},\binom{m-1}{b-1}\}$.
\textup{(i)} $V_{\rm branch}(d,b)\le V_{\rm fixed}(d,b)\le K_{m,b}V_{\rm branch}(d,b)$.
\textup{(ii)} At every law $P$ of $(X,Y)$,
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm branch}(P)\le W_{\rm fixed}(P)\le K_{m,b}\,W_{\rm branch}(P).\label{eq:universal}
\end{equation}
\textup{(iii)} The address contrast on $m=b-1+2^{b-1}$ coordinates has $V_{\rm fixed}/V_{\rm branch}=2^{b-1}$, and $W_{\rm fixed}(P)/W_{\rm branch}(P)=2^{b-1}$ at uniform profiles for every accuracy of the rule in $(0,1)$. For every $b\ge2$ there is an example with $m\le b^2$ and ratio at least $2^{b-b/(\kappa+1)-\kappa}$, $\kappa=\lfloor\log_2 b\rfloor$.
\end{theorem}
The bound $2^{b-1}$ is thus attained once $m$ is exponential in $b$ and nearly attained in polynomial dimension; for $m$ close to $b$ the exact extreme is open (at $(m,b)=(4,3)$ it lies in $[2.45,3]$, Proposition~\ref{prop:fourbit}).

(b) Path guessing: a fixed form draws one of a tree's at most $2^{b-1}$ padded path sets and rescales the tree's centered score by $1/q_S$ when the path completes inside it; at a population, $q_S\propto v_S^{1/2}$ gives the effective number of paths $N_P$. (c) Binary pointer tree: $L=b-1$ levels of pointer bits select a child and the target is the data bit at the reached leaf; a round that reads $a$ pointer bits raises the conditional probability of the most likely leaf by at most a factor $1+2a$.}

\paragraph{Proof idea.}
Pad every root-to-leaf path of a tree to $b$ distinct coordinates. Its first $b-1$ answers determine the path's coordinate set, so a tree has at most $K_{m,b}$ distinct sets. A fixed form draws one set uniformly, follows the tree when it can, and multiplies a completed score by the number of sets (Figure~\ref{fig:constructions}b); the mean is unchanged and the second moment grows by at most $K_{m,b}$. This proves (i). At a population with $\Delta(P)\ne0$ the same simulation inflates the variance by more than $K_{m,b}$; part (ii) follows by simulating the \emph{centered} score: return $c+K(H-c)$ on completion and $c$ otherwise, with the constant $c=\Delta(P)$, which is unbiased for every $c$ and multiplies the variance by at most $K_{m,b}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:population}). For sharpness, let $\kappa=b-1$ address bits select one of $D=2^\kappa$ data bits, $d(A,Z)=Z_A$ (Figure~\ref{fig:constructions}a; the worked example is $\kappa=1$). Branching computes $d$. Under the uniform law, a form $T$ with $s$ address and $\lambda$ data bits explains energy
\begin{equation}
 \|\E[d\mid X_T]\|_2^2=\lambda2^{s-2\kappa}\le2^{-\kappa},\qquad \lambda+s\le\kappa+1,\label{eq:addressinformation}
\end{equation}
and the dual in Theorem~\ref{thm:char} gives $V_{\rm fixed}\ge D$. The same energy bounds the variance that a form explains when the label agrees with $d$ with any probability $p\in(0,1)$, so the gap does not rely on chance-level rules (Proposition~\ref{prop:labelnoise}). Products of address blocks give the polynomial-dimensional example (Appendix~\ref{app:productaddress}). The gap requires that \emph{every} record obey the cap: under a mean budget of $b$ queries, querying all $m$ features with probability $b/m$ bounds the ratio by $m/b$ (Appendix~\ref{app:expectedproof}).

\begin{theorem}[Rounds of adaptivity]\label{thm:rounds}
Let $V_r$ and $W_r(P)$ denote the constants for $r$-round interviews with at most $b$ queries, and $K_r=\min\{2^{b-r},\binom{m-r}{b-r}\}$.
\textup{(a)} For every nonzero contrast of degree at most $b$, every law $P$ and $1\le r\le b$: $V_r\le K_rV_{\rm branch}$ and $W_r(P)\le K_rW_{\rm branch}(P)$.
\textup{(b)} For $b\ge2$, the binary pointer tree $B_{b-1}$ on $m=2^b-1$ coordinates satisfies $V_{\rm branch}=1$ and
\[
 V_r(B_{b-1})\ \ge\ \frac{2^{b-1}}{(1+2b/r)^{r}}\qquad(1\le r\le b).
\]
\textup{(c)} For $b\ge4$ there is a contrast of degree at most $b$ on $m\le b^2$ coordinates, a product of pointer trees, with $V_{\rm branch}=1$ and $\log_2V_r\ge b-O(rb\log\log b/\log b)$.
The lower bounds hold for $W_r(P)/W_{\rm branch}(P)$ at uniform profiles and every accuracy of the rule.
\end{theorem}

Part (a) follows the tree for $r-1$ rounds and guesses the rest of the path in the last batch. The binary pointer tree is the tree evaluation instance of \citet{cook2012}, for which \citet{li2026regex} show that exact evaluation with $B$ queries per round needs $L/\lceil\log_2(B+1)\rceil$ rounds; under a total per-record cap exact evaluation in few rounds is impossible, and the question is how much variance the shortfall costs. Let $M_t$ be $2^{L}$ times the conditional probability of the most likely leaf after $t$ rounds and $\pi_t$ the conditional probability that a data bit already read is the selected one. A round that reads $n_t$ coordinates satisfies $\E[M_t+2^L\pi_t\mid\text{past}]\le(1+2n_t)(M_{t-1}+2^L\pi_{t-1})$: a newly read pointer can at most double the probability of a leaf below it, and a newly read data bit is the selected one with probability at most $M_{t-1}2^{-L}$. Since the potential starts at one and the probability of having read the selected bit is at most $2^{-L}$ times its final value, that probability is at most $2^{-L}\prod_t(1+2n_t)\le2^{-L}(1+2b/r)^r$, and the dual of Theorem~\ref{thm:char} with the witness $h=d$ turns it into (b). The potential is multiplicative across independent blocks even when an interview splits its questions among them adaptively, which gives (c) (Appendix~\ref{app:roundshier}; a pointer-chasing contrast there separates $r$ from $r+1$ rounds). Three consequences follow. For every fixed $r$ the exponent of the $r$-round gap is $b-O(r\log b)$, against the upper bound $b-r$. A protocol within a factor $b^c$ of branching on every contrast needs $r\log_2(1+2b/r)\ge b-1-c\log_2b$, hence $r\ge0.37b-(1+c\log_2b)$ rounds; whether $b-O(\log b)$ rounds are needed is open. And exponential dimension is not needed. \section{The Value of Adaptivity at a Population}\label{sec:population}
The worst-case constants are attained by extremal populations. For a fixed $P$ we bracket the records ratio by interpretable quantities. Let $\tau$ be a depth-$b$ tree that computes $d$, let $S_\tau(x)$ be the padded coordinate set of the path of $x$, $\sigma_P^2=\Var_P(t)>0$ and $v_S=\E_P[(t-\Delta)^2\mathbf 1\{S_\tau(X)=S\}]/\sigma_P^2$. Define
\begin{gather*}
 \rho_P^2=\max_{|T|=b}\Var_P\big(\E_P[t\mid X_T,Y]\big)\big/\sigma_P^2,\\
 N_P(\tau)=\Big(\sum\nolimits_Sv_S^{1/2}\Big)^2.
\end{gather*}
$N_P$ is the exponential R\'enyi entropy of order $1/2$ of the path weights, the effective number of question sets the tree uses at $P$; $\rho_P^2$ is the largest share of the target's variance that any single form explains, a population $R^2$.

\begin{theorem}[Records bracket at a population]\label{thm:bracket}
If a depth-$b$ tree $\tau$ computes $d$, then $W_{\rm branch}(P)=\sigma_P^2$ and
\[
 \frac1{\rho_P^2}\ \le\ \frac{W_{\rm fixed}(P)}{W_{\rm branch}(P)}\ \le\ N_P(\tau)\ \le\ K_{m,b}.
\]
All three inequalities are equalities for the address contrast under the uniform law. For every menu $\A$, constant $c$ and witness $h$, $W_\A(P)+(\Delta-c)^2\ge\E_P[(t-c)h]^2/\max_{\iota\in\A}\E_P[\E_P[h\mid\ell_\iota(X),Y]^2]$.
\end{theorem}
The upper bound is centered path guessing with $q_S\propto v_S^{1/2}$, and the lower bound is Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the decomposition $\sum_\iota g_\iota=t$ (Appendix~\ref{app:population}). Adaptivity pays when the tree's questions diverge over many well-populated paths and no fixed set of $b$ questions explains most of the target's variance. Both ends are computable from reference marginals of $b$ items and the label, and optimizing the scores over the tree's path sets alone gives a tighter computable upper end $W_{\rm PS}(P)\ge W_{\rm fixed}(P)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:upper}).

\paragraph{Computing every protocol class exactly.}
Pad every transcript to exactly $b$ coordinates, so that an interview is a partition of the cube into cells $\{x:x_T=u\}$ with $|T|=b$. On the cube, pointwise unbiasedness of the tables is equivalent to matching the Fourier coefficients of $t$ of degree at most $b$, so \eqref{eq:mainW} over a list of interviews is a second-order cone program in the tables and the interview probabilities, and the masses of all $\binom mb2^{b+1}$ cells are computed once from records. Column generation solves a restricted master problem over a few interviews. For its dual witness $\eta$, the information of a cell $(T,u)$ is a Walsh transform of $\eta$ restricted to $T$, and the most informative interview of any class $\A_r$ is found by dynamic programming over partial assignments: a batch $B\supsetneq A$ is chosen to maximize the summed value of its extensions, singleton batches give trees, and one full batch gives forms. Any normalized witness certifies a lower bound through the last display of Theorem~\ref{thm:bracket}, and the master value is an upper bound. One iteration costs $O(\binom mb4^b)$ and never touches the $2^m$ profiles (Appendix~\ref{app:engine}); in design language this is a vertex-direction method whose direction step is solved exactly over a combinatorial design space \citep{fedorov1972,wynn1970}.

\paragraph{Designs that can be deployed.}
The unbiasedness identities hold at every profile, so a design fitted on reference marginals stays exactly unbiased when the reference is wrong; only its variance depends on the reference. The same holds for a design learned from the audit itself. If a pilot of $n_1\to\infty$, $n_1=o(n)$, records is interviewed with the uniform design, the cell masses are estimated from it with vanishing smoothing, and the remaining records use the design that solves \eqref{eq:mainW} at the estimates, then the estimator is exactly unbiased for every population and $n$ times its variance converges to $W_\A(P)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:selfcal}); Section~\ref{sec:experiments} measures the cost at finite pilot sizes. Since labels are observed before the questions are asked, the form probabilities may also depend on the label, as in outcome-dependent two-phase sampling. This leaves $V$ unchanged, the program decouples over the two labels, and Theorem~\ref{thm:universal}(ii) continues to hold (Proposition~\ref{prop:labeldesign}); the same bound holds for several contrasts served by one design (Appendix~\ref{app:population}).

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Adaptivity in query models.} Within-input adaptivity is classical in decision-tree and property-testing complexity, with round hierarchies for testers \citep{canonne2018}, pointer chasing in communication \citep{nisan1993} and round--query tradeoffs for evaluating a pointer tree exactly \citep{cook2012,li2026regex}; these count queries or rounds on a worst-case input, whereas we measure the variance that a shortfall of rounds costs under a per-record cap. In the huge-object model, adaptive and non-adaptive testers of long strings are separated by query complexity \citep{goldreich2022,chakraborty2023}, and the locally bounded model of \citet{adar2024}, which adapts within a sample and passes nothing between samples, is the testing counterpart of our branching class and is exponentially separated from the non-adaptive one. The qualitative separation is therefore known; exact variance constants for estimation, their validity at every population and the round hierarchy on the variance scale are not implied by these results. Horvitz--Thompson weighting over random query paths appears in lower bounds for adaptive relaxed locally decodable codes \citep{goldberg2026}.

\paragraph{Interaction in information-constrained estimation.} Estimation under communication and local-privacy constraints fixes a per-sample channel family \citep{acharya2019,barnes2020}; in local differential privacy, pointer chasing separates numbers of rounds \citep{joseph2019,joseph2020}, and for learning and testing distributions interaction across users does not help \citep{acharya2022tit,lau2026}. These results concern noisy channels and sample complexity up to constants. Ours is a noiseless analogue with exact constants: within-record adaptivity is worth the constants of Theorems~\ref{thm:universal}--\ref{thm:bracket}, while adaptation across records leaves the first-order constant unchanged, in line with adaption results for linear problems \citep{bakhvalov1971,novak1996}.

\paragraph{Optimal design and design-based minimaxity.} For one menu, our population program is $c$-optimal design \citep{elfving1952,pukelsheim1993}: the witness bound is its Elfving dual and column generation a vertex-direction algorithm \citep{fedorov1972,wynn1970,sagnol2011}. Minimax pairs of a sampling design and a design-unbiased estimator are classical for fully observed units \citep{stenger1979,gabler1990,aronow2026}. These theories optimize within one design space; they do not compare the spaces induced by pre-committed, $r$-round and branching queries, which is the subject here.

\paragraph{Missing data by design, two-phase sampling and adaptive testing.} Split questionnaires and matrix sampling assign fixed forms \citep{raghunathan1995,graham2006,thomas2006,chipperfield2009,rhemtulla2012}, and known-coarsening theory gives efficient augmented estimators for a fixed coarsening mechanism \citep{robins1994,tsiatis2006}; our form program computes that augmentation jointly with the form probabilities. Adaptive matrix sampling sets the probabilities of a respondent's later sub-questionnaires from the first interview and reports efficiency gains on historical data \citep{gonzalez2008}; it is a two-round interview in our sense. Two-phase designs choose from a unit's first-phase values whether to measure an expensive block, with optimal and pilot-based adaptive selection probabilities for mean-score and efficient estimators \citep{reilly1995,gilbert2014,mcisaac2015,tao2020,chen2020,chen2022}; in our terms they are two-round interviews with one binary decision under an expected budget, whereas we study which of $m$ items to reveal under a hard cap and how the answer changes with the number of rounds. Multistage adaptive tests route students between blocks in rounds \citep{yamamoto2018,fink2024}. These lines optimize one protocol under a model; we compare protocol classes at their minimax optima without a response model.

\paragraph{Evaluation and acquisition in ML.} Restricted-focus-of-attention learning lets a learner choose a few attributes of each example before seeing it, which is our fixed-form class, and studies which concepts remain learnable \citep{bendavid1998}; attribute-efficient and budgeted learning fit predictors from such views \citep{cesa2011,hazan2012,lizotte2003,kapoor2005}. Active testing and prediction-powered inference allocate labels \citep{kossen2021,zrnic2024,angelopoulos2023}, the reverse of our cost structure. Active feature acquisition chooses features for individual predictions \citep{trapeznikov2013,nan2017,covert2023}, and the value of such a policy is estimated from retrospective data \citep{vonkleist2025}.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
Within-record adaptivity is worth at most $2^{b-1}$ in records at every population, and this is sharp; it accrues round by round, and in the worst case, already in polynomial dimension, nearly all of it remains out of reach of any constant number of rounds. The framework covers known low-degree functionals fixed before collection, not the many estimands of a multipurpose survey: records collected for one rule can be nearly useless for another, although a joint design for a few known rules is cheap (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}). Its guarantees are design-based, and the population constants compare designs tuned to the population in the limit of many records per cell; at survey sizes of a few thousand records a parsimonious model for the capped records is more accurate, and a complete reference that transports is more accurate still. It assumes that answers do not depend on question order or mode, that requested answers are returned, and that every sampled label is observed. Order effects are the first-order practical obstacle: \nOrderSentence{} The identification results concern finite domains and low-degree contrasts; thresholded or link-transformed ensembles often exceed small caps, and their identification floors must be certified first (Appendix~\ref{app:workflow}). Population-specific designs use the target law, which a fitted design approximates from reference records or a pilot at the costs reported above. For ensembles the cap itself, not pre-commitment, is the larger cost (Table~\ref{tab:capgrid}), and when the cap can be relaxed and labels are expensive, complete records can be cheaper than any capped design (Appendix~\ref{app:costchoice}). The populations are unweighted empirical laws, sparse and smoothed for the forest tasks, so the constants are exact for those laws and carry sampling error as estimates for the underlying populations. The solver's cost grows as $\binom mb4^b$ per iteration; we ran fixed designs for $m\le21$ and $r$-round and branching designs for $m\le12$, $b\le6$, and four two-round programs at $b=6$ did not close. The exact frontier of $V_{\rm fixed}/V_{\rm branch}$ at fixed $(m,b)$, the factor $(1+2b/r)^r$ of the round hierarchy, and the first-order constant of the biased regime remain open.

\section*{Impact Statement}
Shorter adaptive questionnaires and test sequences can reduce respondent and patient burden. Branching exposes people to different questions; order effects, selective nonresponse and subgroup-specific burdens can bias estimates or hide unequal outcomes, and population-average gains do not imply subgroup gains, so deployments should monitor both.
