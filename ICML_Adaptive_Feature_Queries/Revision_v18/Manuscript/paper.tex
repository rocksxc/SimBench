\begin{abstract}
Rotated questionnaire booklets, laboratory panels and budgeted classifiers reveal at most $b$ of $m$ features per record. When each record's questions are fixed in advance instead of chosen from its earlier answers, how many more records does an estimate of a known low-degree functional, such as the accuracy of a frozen screening rule, need? The answer depends on what is assumed about the unasked features. Without a model, pre-committed forms need at most $2^{b-1}$ times the records of branching interviews at every population, with equality at populations of any accuracy; $r$ rounds of questions need at most $2^{b-r}$, and a constant number of rounds can still lose $2^{b(1-o(1))}$. The bound comes from an exact simulation of branching by forms, so it holds for every model, estimator and sample size. Whether a model removes the gap depends on dependence and not on parsimony alone: under a log-linear model with interactions of order at most $k$ the gap is at most $(b+1)^k$ at and near uniform laws, and it is exactly one for the extremal contrast under the pairwise model, yet pairwise laws with strong couplings bring back $2^{b-1}/b$. All constants are computed with certificates by column generation over forms, $r$-round and branching interviews. \nAbstractData
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Population quantities are often estimated from records that each reveal only a few features. Questionnaires cap respondent burden, clinical protocols cap the tests per patient, and budgeted classifiers acquire a few inputs per case \citep{turney1995,trapeznikov2013,nan2017}. A collector can pre-commit to each record's feature set, as in split questionnaires and laboratory panels ordered at once \citep{raghunathan1995,chipperfield2009}, or choose each question after seeing earlier answers, as in skip logic, adaptive matrix sampling \citep{gonzalez2008} and reflex testing. This paper asks how many more records pre-commitment costs, and how much of the loss a few rounds of questions recover, when the target is a population quantity such as the accuracy of a frozen screening rule or the accuracy gain of a model update.

The answer depends on what the analyst assumes about the features that were not asked, and we treat both ends. In the \emph{design-based} regime nothing is assumed: the label of every sampled record is observed and charged separately, the target is one known contrast of degree at most $b$, answers do not depend on question order, and estimators are unbiased by design. In the \emph{model-based} regime the law of the features and the label lies in a parsimonious model, such as a log-linear model with low-order interactions, and the estimator is maximum likelihood. Design-based claims hold whatever the population; model-based estimates are more accurate at survey sizes when the model is adequate. We give the price of pre-commitment in both regimes and measure it on trained rules.

That adaptivity within one input can be worth exponentially many queries is classical in decision-tree complexity, the huge-object model and interactive local privacy \citep{goldreich2022,adar2024,joseph2019,joseph2020}. Under a hard per-record cap the price is paid in records, and the questions become quantitative: what is the exact price, how much of it do a few rounds recover, and what remains of it at a given population or under a model?

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt\parskip0pt
\item \emph{Exact constants without a model (Theorems~\ref{thm:char}, \ref{thm:universal}).} The first-order minimax constant of any finite menu of interviews is a convex program. Forms simulate branching exactly with $K_{m,b}=\min\{2^{b-1},\binom{m-1}{b-1}\}$ times the records, for every task and at \emph{every} population, and an address contrast attains $2^{b-1}$ at populations of every accuracy.
\item \emph{Rounds (Theorem~\ref{thm:rounds}).} Interviews with $r$ batches need at most $2^{b-r}$ times the records of branching. A pointer tree on $2^b-1$ features loses $2^{b-1}/(1+2b/r)^r$, so polynomial competitiveness takes more than $0.37b-O(\log b)$ rounds there; on at most $b^2$ features, products still lose $2^{b(1-o(1))}$ for every constant $r$.
\item \emph{What a model changes (Theorem~\ref{thm:structured}).} The same factors bound the gap within every model. Under a log-linear model with interactions of order at most $k$ the gap is at most $(b+1)^k$ at and near uniform laws; for the address contrast it is exactly one for $k=2$, at least linear in $b$ for $k=3$, and $2^{b-1}$ at $k=b+1$. Pairwise laws with strong couplings restore $2^{b-1}/b$.
\item \emph{Computation and trained rules (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).} Column generation with exact dynamic-programming pricing certifies the optimal design of every protocol class, with or without a model. \nContribData
\end{itemize}

\section{Estimand and Acquisition Protocols}\label{sec:setup}
Let $X$ take values in $\X=\{-1,1\}^m$ and let $Y\in\{-1,1\}$ be a label. Freeze two rules $p_0,p_1:\X\to[0,1]$ that predict $+1$ with probabilities $p_j(x)$, put $d=p_1-p_0$, $t(x,y)=yd(x)$, and
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(P)=\E_P[t(X,Y)]=\E_P[Yd(X)].\label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
This is the accuracy difference of the two rules; $p_0\equiv1/2$ makes it affine in the accuracy of $p_1$ alone (other proper scores: Appendix~\ref{app:metrics}). Labels are recorded for every sampled record and charged separately; the cap $b$ bounds the feature queries per record and is \emph{binding} when $m>b$.

\paragraph{Protocols.}
An interview $\iota$ maps a profile $x$ to a transcript $\ell_\iota(x)$. A \emph{fixed form} queries a coordinate set chosen before the record is seen. An \emph{$r$-round interview} queries batches $B_1,B_2(x_{B_1}),\ldots,B_r$, each chosen from the answers to earlier batches, with $\sum_s|B_s|\le b$. A \emph{branching interview} is a decision tree of depth at most $b$. The menus satisfy $\A_{\rm fixed}=\A_1\subseteq\A_2\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\A_b=\A_{\rm branch}$. In missing-data terms a fixed form is missingness completely at random by design, and an $r$-round or branching interview is a randomized monotone coarsening \citep{gill1997}: ignorable, with a pattern that depends on the answers already given. Across records, a protocol may use all previous records to choose the next interview, and our lower bounds even allow the current label to be seen first. $R_\A(d,n)$ is the minimax mean squared error over protocols and estimators that use $n$ records and interviews from $\A$.

\paragraph{Identification.}
Let $\Hh_\A$ be the span of functions constant on the transcript cells of $\A$. The contrast is identifiable for all populations exactly when $d\in\Hh_\A$; for fixed forms of size $b$ and for depth-$b$ trees this is the same condition, Fourier degree $\deg(d)\le b$ in the characters $\chi_S(x)=\prod_{j\in S}x_j$. It holds for every probability average of depth-$b$ trees, such as a depth-limited random forest, and fails for most thresholded ensembles at small caps.

\paragraph{A worked example.}
Let $m=3$, $b=2$ and $d(x)=[(1+x_1)x_2+(1-x_1)x_3]/2$: the first answer selects which of two others is the target (Figure~\ref{fig:queries}). Branching reads $x_1$ and then the selected coordinate, so it observes $t$. Under uniform profiles and an independent uniform label, every form of size two explains half of the variance of $t$, and the best pre-committed design draws $\{1,2\}$ or $\{1,3\}$ with probability $1/2$ and doubles the score when the drawn form contains the selected coordinate: $W_{\rm fixed}/W_{\rm branch}=2=K_{3,2}$.

\section{An Exact Characterization}\label{sec:general}
Choose interview probabilities $q_\iota$ and transcript tables $g_\iota$. The score $Yg_\iota(\ell_\iota(X))/q_\iota$ is unbiased for $Yd(X)$ at every profile exactly when $\sum_\iota g_\iota(\ell_\iota(x))=d(x)$ for all $x$. Define
\begin{equation}
 V(d,\A)=\inf_{\substack{q,g:\ \sum_\iota g_\iota=d}}\ \max_{x\in\X}
             \sum_\iota\frac{g_\iota(\ell_\iota(x))^2}{q_\iota},\label{eq:unbiasedprogram}
\end{equation}
where a zero-probability interview has a zero table.

\begin{theorem}[Exact characterization]\label{thm:char}
Let $\X$ and $\A$ be finite and $d\in\Hh_\A$. Then $nR_\A(d,n)\to V(d,\A)$, and an iid design with the unbiased score attains $V/n$ for every $n$. The converse applies to biased estimators, to designs that adapt across records, and to interview choices that depend on the current label. Moreover
\[
 V(d,\A)=\sup_{\mu,h}\frac{\langle d,h\rangle_\mu^2}{\max_{\iota\in\A}\|\E_\mu[h\mid\ell_\iota(X)]\|_\mu^2},
\]
over strictly positive profile laws $\mu$ and witnesses $h$ with nonzero denominator.
\end{theorem}
The dual compares the signal $\langle d,h\rangle_\mu$ of a perturbation of the label mean with the largest projection energy that one interview can capture (proof and a finite-sample version in Appendix~\ref{app:B}). The ingredients are standard: minimax duality for linear functionals \citep{donoho1994,juditsky2009,polyanskiy2026} and information bounds that hold simultaneously over a family of channels, including sequentially chosen ones \citep{acharya2019,barnes2020}. We use them to obtain \emph{exact} constants for each query class, which a comparison of classes requires.

\paragraph{Population-specific constants.}
For a fixed law $P$ of $(X,Y)$, let
\begin{equation}
 W_{\A}(P)=\inf_{q,H:\,\E[H\mid X,Y]=t(X,Y)}\Var_{P,q}(H),\label{eq:mainW}
\end{equation}
where the score $H$ of the drawn interview's transcript and the label must be unbiased at every profile and label but may be tuned to $P$. At a full-support $P$, $W_\A(P)/n$ is the local asymptotic minimax risk over arbitrary estimators that use the capped records alone (Proposition~\ref{prop:local}), so $W_{\rm fixed}(P)/W_{\rm branch}(P)$ is the ratio of records the two classes need near $P$ when $n$ is large relative to the number of cells and each class uses its best design for $P$. For a fixed menu, \eqref{eq:mainW} is a $c$-optimal design problem whose design points are interviews, with an Elfving-type dual, and for a fixed design its score is the efficient augmented inverse-probability estimator under known coarsening \citep{elfving1952,pukelsheim1993,robins1994,tsiatis2006}. What is new is the comparison \emph{across} menus and the exact optimization over the combinatorial menus of $r$-round and branching interviews.

\section{How Much Can Adaptivity Be Worth?}\label{sec:gap}
\begin{theorem}[The gap and its extremes]\label{thm:universal}
Let $1\le b\le m$ and let $d$ be a nonzero contrast of degree at most $b$ on $\{-1,1\}^m$, with $K_{m,b}=\min\{2^{b-1},\binom{m-1}{b-1}\}$.
\textup{(i)} Every protocol on $n$ branching records, with any estimator, is reproduced with the same law at every population by a fixed-form protocol on a random number of records with mean $K_{m,b}n$ (label-dependent only if the original is).
\textup{(ii)} Consequently $V_{\rm branch}(d,b)\le V_{\rm fixed}(d,b)\le K_{m,b}V_{\rm branch}(d,b)$, and at every law $P$ of $(X,Y)$,
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm branch}(P)\le W_{\rm fixed}(P)\le K_{m,b}\,W_{\rm branch}(P).\label{eq:universal}
\end{equation}
\textup{(iii)} The address contrast on $m=b-1+2^{b-1}$ coordinates has $V_{\rm fixed}/V_{\rm branch}=2^{b-1}$, and $W_{\rm fixed}(P)/W_{\rm branch}(P)=2^{b-1}$ at uniform profiles for every accuracy of the rule in $(0,1)$. For every $b\ge2$ there is an example with $m\le b^2$ and ratio at least $2^{b-b/(\kappa+1)-\kappa}$, $\kappa=\lfloor\log_2 b\rfloor$.
\end{theorem}
The bound $2^{b-1}$ is thus attained once $m$ is exponential in $b$ and nearly attained in polynomial dimension; for $m$ close to $b$ the extreme is open (Proposition~\ref{prop:fourbit}).

\paragraph{Proof idea.}
Pad every root-to-leaf path of a tree to $b$ distinct coordinates. Its first $b-1$ answers determine the path's coordinate set, so a tree has at most $K_{m,b}$ distinct sets. Draw one of them uniformly and ask it as a form (Figure~\ref{fig:constructions}b). For every profile exactly one set is the right one, so with the same probability for every record, at least $1/K$, the form contains the transcript that the tree would have produced. Discarding the other records simulates the branching experiment exactly, for every estimator, loss and sample size (Theorem~\ref{thm:simulation}); this is the noiseless counterpart of converting fully into sequentially interactive private protocols \citep{joseph2019}. With a fixed number of records the designs we compute use an explicit unbiased score instead: return $c+K(H-c)$ on completion and $c$ otherwise, with $c=\Delta(P)$, which multiplies the variance of the tree's score $H$ by at most $K_{m,b}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:population}). For sharpness, let $\kappa=b-1$ address bits select one of $D=2^\kappa$ data bits, $d(A,Z)=Z_A$ (Figure~\ref{fig:constructions}a; the worked example is $\kappa=1$). Branching computes $d$. Under the uniform law, a form $T$ with $s$ address and $\lambda$ data bits explains energy
\begin{equation}
 \|\E[d\mid X_T]\|_2^2=\lambda2^{s-2\kappa}\le2^{-\kappa},\qquad \lambda+s\le\kappa+1,\label{eq:addressinformation}
\end{equation}
and the dual in Theorem~\ref{thm:char} gives $V_{\rm fixed}\ge D$. The same energy bounds the variance that a form explains when the label agrees with $d$ with any probability $p\in(0,1)$, so the gap does not rely on chance-level rules (Proposition~\ref{prop:labelnoise}). Products of address blocks give the polynomial-dimensional example (Appendix~\ref{app:productaddress}). The gap requires that \emph{every} record obey the cap: under a mean budget of $b$ queries, querying all $m$ features with probability $b/m$ bounds the ratio by $m/b$ (Appendix~\ref{app:expectedproof}).

\begin{theorem}[Rounds of adaptivity]\label{thm:rounds}
Let $V_r$ and $W_r(P)$ denote the constants for $r$-round interviews with at most $b$ queries, and $K_r=\min\{2^{b-r},\binom{m-r}{b-r}\}$.
\textup{(a)} For every nonzero contrast of degree at most $b$, every law $P$ and $1\le r\le b$: $V_r\le K_rV_{\rm branch}$ and $W_r(P)\le K_rW_{\rm branch}(P)$.
\textup{(b)} For $b\ge2$, the binary pointer tree $B_{b-1}$ on $m=2^b-1$ coordinates satisfies $V_{\rm branch}=1$ and
\[
 V_r(B_{b-1})\ \ge\ \frac{2^{b-1}}{(1+2b/r)^{r}}\qquad(1\le r\le b).
\]
\textup{(c)} For $b\ge4$ there is a contrast of degree at most $b$ on $m\le b^2$ coordinates, a product of pointer trees, with $V_{\rm branch}=1$ and $\log_2V_r\ge b-O(rb\log\log b/\log b)$.
The lower bounds hold for $W_r(P)/W_{\rm branch}(P)$ at uniform profiles and every accuracy of the rule.
\end{theorem}

Part (a) is the simulation of Theorem~\ref{thm:universal}(i) after following the tree for $r-1$ rounds. The binary pointer tree is the tree evaluation instance of \citet{cook2012}; round hierarchies for query cost under bounded adaptivity are known in property testing and oracle evaluation \citep{canonne2018,li2026regex}. Under a total per-record cap exact evaluation in few rounds is impossible, and the question is how much variance the shortfall costs. For (b), let $M_t$ be $2^{L}$ times the conditional probability of the most likely leaf after $t$ rounds and $\pi_t$ the conditional probability that a data bit already read is the selected one. A round that reads $n_t$ coordinates multiplies the expected potential $M_t+2^L\pi_t$ by at most $1+2n_t$, so the selected bit is read with probability at most $2^{-L}(1+2b/r)^r$, and the dual of Theorem~\ref{thm:char} with the witness $h=d$ gives (b). The potential is multiplicative across independent blocks, which gives (c) (Appendix~\ref{app:roundshier}).

The consequences differ in the dimension they need. On the $2^b-1$ features of the pointer tree the exponent of the $r$-round gap is $b-O(r\log b)$ for fixed $r$, against the upper bound $b-r$, and a protocol within a factor $b^c$ of branching needs $r\log_2(1+2b/r)\ge b-1-c\log_2b$, hence $r\ge0.37b-(1+c\log_2b)$ rounds. In polynomial dimension less is proved: the product of part (c) loses $2^{b(1-o(1))}$ for every constant $r$, but reading one level of every block per round evaluates it exactly in $\lfloor\log_2b\rfloor$ rounds, so it shows a loss only for $r=o(\log b/\log\log b)$; whether more than logarithmically many rounds can be necessary on $\mathrm{poly}(b)$ features is open. Bound (b) exceeds one for two rounds once $b\ge8$; at small caps the solver of Section~\ref{sec:population} brackets $V_r(B_{b-1})$ between its value at the uniform law and the worst profile of the uniform-optimal design: \nPointerSentence{}

\section{The Value of Adaptivity at a Population}\label{sec:population}
For a fixed $P$ we bracket the records ratio by interpretable quantities. Let $\tau$ be a depth-$b$ tree that computes $d$, let $S_\tau(x)$ be the padded coordinate set of the path of $x$, $\sigma_P^2=\Var_P(t)>0$ and $v_S=\E_P[(t-\Delta)^2\mathbf 1\{S_\tau(X)=S\}]/\sigma_P^2$. Define
\begin{gather*}
 \rho_P^2=\max_{|T|=b}\Var_P\big(\E_P[t\mid X_T,Y]\big)\big/\sigma_P^2,\\
 N_P(\tau)=\Big(\sum\nolimits_Sv_S^{1/2}\Big)^2.
\end{gather*}
$N_P$ is the exponential R\'enyi entropy of order $1/2$ of the path weights, the effective number of question sets the tree uses at $P$; $\rho_P^2$ is the largest share of the target's variance that any single form explains, a population $R^2$.

\begin{theorem}[Records bracket at a population]\label{thm:bracket}
If a depth-$b$ tree $\tau$ computes $d$, then $W_{\rm branch}(P)=\sigma_P^2$ and
\[
 \frac1{\rho_P^2}\ \le\ \frac{W_{\rm fixed}(P)}{W_{\rm branch}(P)}\ \le\ N_P(\tau)\ \le\ K_{m,b}.
\]
All three inequalities are equalities for the address contrast under the uniform law. For every menu $\A$, constant $c$ and witness $h$, $W_\A(P)+(\Delta-c)^2\ge\E_P[(t-c)h]^2/\max_{\iota\in\A}\E_P[\E_P[h\mid\ell_\iota(X),Y]^2]$.
\end{theorem}
The upper bound is centered path guessing with $q_S\propto v_S^{1/2}$, and the lower bound is Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the decomposition $\sum_\iota g_\iota=t$ (Appendix~\ref{app:population}). Adaptivity pays when the tree's questions diverge over many well-populated paths and no fixed set of $b$ questions explains most of the target's variance.

\paragraph{Computing every protocol class exactly.}
Pad every transcript to exactly $b$ coordinates, so that an interview is a partition of the cube into cells $\{x:x_T=u\}$ with $|T|=b$. Pointwise unbiasedness of the tables is then equivalent to matching the Fourier coefficients of $t$ of degree at most $b$, and \eqref{eq:mainW} over a list of interviews is a second-order cone program in the tables and the interview probabilities. Column generation solves a restricted master problem over a few interviews; for its dual witness, the information of a cell is a Walsh transform restricted to $T$, and the most informative interview of any class $\A_r$ is found by dynamic programming over partial assignments (singleton batches give trees, one full batch gives forms). Any witness certifies a lower bound through the last display of Theorem~\ref{thm:bracket}, and the master value is an upper bound. One iteration costs $O(\binom mb4^b)$ and never touches the $2^m$ profiles (Appendix~\ref{app:engine}); the same column generation, with the witness restricted to a tangent space, computes the constants of Section~\ref{sec:model}.

\paragraph{Designs that can be deployed.}
The unbiasedness identities hold at every profile, so a design fitted on reference marginals, or learned from a pilot of the audit itself, stays exactly unbiased when the reference is wrong; only its variance depends on it. With a pilot of $n_1\to\infty$, $n_1=o(n)$ records, $n$ times the variance converges to $W_\A(P)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:selfcal}); Section~\ref{sec:experiments} charges the pilot at finite sizes. Form probabilities may also depend on the observed label, as in outcome-dependent two-phase sampling, without changing Theorem~\ref{thm:universal} (Proposition~\ref{prop:labeldesign}).

\section{What a Model Changes}\label{sec:model}
The constants so far are for an analyst who assumes nothing about the unasked features. Suppose instead that the law of $(X,Y)$ lies in a smooth parametric model with tangent space $\mathcal T$ at $P$. The records of an interview $\iota$ carry the information $J_\iota(h)=\E_P[\E_P[h\mid\ell_\iota(X),Y]^2]$ about a direction $h\in\mathcal T$, and the local asymptotic minimax constant of a menu \emph{within the model} is
\begin{equation}
 W^{\mathcal T}_\A(P)=\min_q\ \sup_{h\in\mathcal T}\ \frac{\langle t-\Delta(P),h\rangle_P^2}{\sum_\iota q_\iota J_\iota(h)},\label{eq:mainWT}
\end{equation}
a $c$-optimal design problem (Proposition~\ref{prop:localmodel}). It is \eqref{eq:mainW} with the dual witness restricted to $\mathcal T$, so a model lowers the constant of every menu; the question is whether it lowers that of fixed forms by more. Let $\mathcal M_k$ be the log-linear model with all interactions of order at most $k$ among the $m$ features and the label, and $W^{(k)}_\A$ its constant: $k=2$ is the pairwise model, and $k=m+1$ is saturated and returns $W_\A$.

\begin{theorem}[Adaptivity in a model]\label{thm:structured}
\textup{(a)} For every model, every law $P$ and $1\le r\le b$: $W^{\mathcal T}_r(P)\le K_r\,W^{\mathcal T}_{\rm branch}(P)$.
\textup{(b)} If the features and the label are independent under $P$, each taking both values with probability at least $\lambda$, then $W^{(k)}_{\rm fixed}(P)\le\min\{K_{m,b},\nu_k\}\,W^{(k)}_{\rm branch}(P)$ with $\nu_k=\sum_{j=1}^k\binom{b+1}j(\frac{1-\lambda}\lambda)^j$, which is at most $(b+1)^k$ at uniform laws; for $k\le b$ the bound holds up to a factor $1+\epsilon$ on a neighbourhood of $P$ in $\mathcal M_k$.
\textup{(c)} For the address contrast at the uniform law, $W^{(2)}_{\rm fixed}=W^{(2)}_{\rm branch}=\sigma_P^2/b$, and for $3\le k\le b+1$ the ratio $W^{(k)}_{\rm fixed}/W^{(k)}_{\rm branch}$ is at least $L_k(b)\ge\binom{b-1}{k-2}/b$, with $L_{b+1}(b)=2^{b-1}$; for $k=3$ it is at least $(4/9+o(1))\,b$.
\textup{(d)} For every $b\ge3$ and $\epsilon>0$ there are full-support laws in the pairwise model $\mathcal M_2$, on $b-1+3\cdot2^{b-1}$ features, at which $W^{(k)}_{\rm fixed}/W^{(k)}_{\rm branch}\ge(1-\epsilon)\,2^{b-1}/b$ for every $k\ge2$.
\end{theorem}
Part (a) is Theorem~\ref{thm:universal}(i), which involves no model. Part (b) is Cauchy--Schwarz over the at most $\nu_k$ low-order monomials inside a path set: without dependence, a direction of low order cannot concentrate on the few profiles whose path a given form completes. Part (c) locates the gap at such laws: for the contrast that attains $2^{b-1}$ without a model, pre-commitment costs nothing under the pairwise model, $2.50$ and $2.75$ at $b=3,4$ and at least linearly more under the three-way model, a polynomial of degree between $k-3$ and $k$ under $\mathcal M_k$, and $2^{b-1}$ again when the model contains the interaction of all $b$ queried items with the label. These are first-order statements; for $k=2$ the optimal designs read only data bits and have singular information (Appendix~\ref{app:structured}). Part (d) shows that parsimony alone is not enough. Add to the address contrast a one-hot copy of the address and the products of its indicators with the data bits, as dummy coding and derived indicators do in a questionnaire, tied by strong pairwise couplings. The target is then a polynomial of degree two on a set of probability nearly one, and forms need $2^{b-1}/b$ times the records of the rule's tree. A model removes the gap when dependence is weak; its order measures parsimony only relative to the coding of the features.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Adaptivity in query models.} Within-input adaptivity is classical in decision-tree and property-testing complexity, with round hierarchies for testers and oracle evaluation \citep{canonne2018,li2026regex} and pointer chasing in communication \citep{nisan1993}; these count queries or rounds on a worst-case input, whereas we measure the variance that a shortfall of rounds costs under a per-record cap. In the huge-object model adaptive and non-adaptive testers are separated by query complexity \citep{goldreich2022,chakraborty2023}; the locally bounded model of \citet{adar2024} is the testing counterpart of our branching class. The qualitative separation is therefore known; exact variance constants, their validity at every population and for every model, and their collapse under low-order structure are not implied.

\paragraph{Information-constrained estimation.} Estimation under communication and local-privacy constraints fixes a per-sample channel family \citep{acharya2019,barnes2020}; pointer chasing separates rounds of interaction in local privacy \citep{joseph2019,joseph2020}, and interaction across users does not help for learning and testing distributions \citep{acharya2022tit,lau2026}. Ours is a noiseless analogue with exact constants; that adaptivity buys little outside worst cases has a precedent in adaptive sensing \citep{ariascastro2013}.

\paragraph{Optimal design and missing data by design.} For one menu our programs are $c$-optimal design and our solver a vertex-direction method \citep{elfving1952,fedorov1972,wynn1970,pukelsheim1993,sagnol2011}; minimax pairs of a design and a design-unbiased estimator are classical for fully observed units \citep{stenger1979,gabler1990,aronow2026}. Split questionnaires and matrix sampling assign fixed forms \citep{raghunathan1995,graham2006,thomas2006,chipperfield2009,rhemtulla2012}, which have been optimized under a model from a pilot or an earlier wave \citep{adiguzel2008,jang2022}, and known-coarsening theory gives the efficient estimator for a fixed mechanism \citep{robins1994,tsiatis2006}. Adaptive matrix sampling \citep{gonzalez2008} and two-phase designs with optimal or pilot-based selection probabilities \citep{reilly1995,gilbert2014,mcisaac2015,tao2020,chen2020,chen2022} are two-round interviews in our sense, as are multistage adaptive tests \citep{yamamoto2018,fink2024}. These lines optimize one protocol within one design space; we compare the spaces induced by pre-committed, $r$-round and branching queries, with and without a model.

\paragraph{Evaluation and acquisition in ML.} Restricted-focus-of-attention learning lets a learner choose a few attributes of each example before seeing it, which is our fixed-form class \citep{bendavid1998}, and attribute-efficient and budgeted learning fit predictors from such views \citep{cesa2011,hazan2012,lizotte2003,kapoor2005}. Active testing and prediction-powered inference allocate labels \citep{kossen2021,zrnic2024,angelopoulos2023}, the reverse of our cost structure, and active feature acquisition chooses features for individual predictions \citep{trapeznikov2013,nan2017,covert2023,vonkleist2025}.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
Within-record adaptivity is worth at most $2^{b-1}$ in records, for every task, model and population, sharply without a model, and it accrues round by round. \emph{Scope.} For a single screening tree the adaptive optimum is the rule itself, so the comparison prices paper forms and panels ordered in one batch against computer-assisted administration of the rule; for ensembles no interview computes the target, and the cap costs more than pre-commitment (Table~\ref{tab:capgrid}). \emph{What the constants mean.} Ratios of $W$ compare designs tuned to the population in the limit of many records per cell, with no sample-size threshold, and the finite-sample theorem carries a factor of 104. The model-based constants hold when the model does. The populations are unweighted, smoothed empirical laws; BRFSS survey weights are ignored. \section*{Impact Statement}
Shorter adaptive questionnaires and test sequences can reduce respondent and patient burden. Branching exposes people to different questions; order effects, selective nonresponse and subgroup-specific burdens can bias estimates or hide unequal outcomes, and population-average gains do not imply subgroup gains, so deployments should monitor both.
