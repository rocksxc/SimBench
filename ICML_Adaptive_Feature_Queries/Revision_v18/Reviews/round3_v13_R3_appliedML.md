**Summary**

The paper asks how many more records a data collector needs when it must pre-commit to which b of m binary features to query for each record, versus choosing each query after seeing earlier answers (a depth-b tree), when the goal is to estimate a population functional Δ(P) = E[Y d(X)] such as the accuracy difference between two frozen classifiers. Labels are always observed and charged separately; the cap applies to features only. Theorem 1 gives the exact first-order minimax constant for any finite menu of query protocols as a finite convex program (eq. 2) with a dual "witness" form, with a converse that holds against biased, across-record-adaptive and label-dependent protocols, plus a finite-sample variant that characterizes minimax risk within a universal constant (104) for identifiable and non-identifiable contrasts alike. Theorem 2 shows the fixed/branching ratio of constants is at most min{2^{b-1}, C(m-1,b-1)}, attained by an "address" contrast (b-1 address bits select one of 2^{b-1} data bits), with a polynomial-dimension near-attainment via products of address blocks, and a ratio of at most m/b under a mean rather than a hard budget. Theorem 3 gives a rounds hierarchy: r batches lose at most 2^{b-r}; pointer chasing separates r from r+1 rounds, and a binary pointer-tree contrast shows r rounds can lose 2^{b-1}/(3b)^r, so Ω(b/log b) rounds are needed to be within polynomial factors of full branching. Theorem 4 brackets the gain at a specific population P between 1/Γ_P (inverse of the largest energy fraction any single form explains) and the Rényi-1/2 effective number of path sets of a tree computing d; a column-generation scheme with exact dynamic-programming pricing computes the branching constant over all depth-b trees with certified bounds; Proposition 5 shows a two-phase pilot design learns the population-optimal design while remaining exactly unbiased. Experiments compute these constants exactly for depth-b random-forest "updates" on WDBC/Spambase/Ionosphere (m ≤ 12, b ≤ 4) and for CART screening trees on BRFSS and thyroid data (b ≤ 6), finding realized fixed/branching record ratios between 1.0 and 1.98, a path-guessing baseline needing up to 25x, and, notably, that multiple imputation or model-assisted estimation from complete source records removes the gap entirely.

**Strengths**

- The question is genuinely new and cleanly posed. Classical adaptivity results (decision-tree complexity, huge-object model, pointer chasing) count queries per input; here the per-record cap is fixed and the price of non-adaptivity is paid in records (variance). Bridging query complexity and statistical efficiency for planned-missingness-style designs is an original framing, and I am not aware of prior work giving sharp constants for this comparison.
- The theory is tight and, as far as I could verify, correct. I checked the duality and two-point/van Trees arguments for Theorems 6–7 (A3; the constant 104 and the 15·27² > 104² check are consistent), the path-set simulation and address energy bound r·2^{s-2κ} ≤ 2^{-κ} for Theorem 2 (A6), the potential inequality E[M_t | T_{t-1}] ≤ (1+2a_t)M_{t-1} and the supermartingale combination in Theorem 20 (A13), the Cauchy–Schwarz bracket in Theorem 22 (A14), and Proposition 24 (A15). The lower bounds hold against biased, sequential and label-dependent protocols, which makes the comparison of protocol classes meaningful.
- The population-specific bracket (Theorem 4) is interpretable (path entropy vs. best single form's explained energy) and is shown empirically to be predictive (Spearman 0.78 on screening trees; the thyroid case where Γ_P = 1 correctly predicts no gain).
- The computational side is unusually rigorous: exact second-order cone programs for the fixed menu, column generation with certified primal/dual gaps (≤ 9e-7) over ~1.5e7 depth-3 trees, sensitivity to smoothing mass and bootstrap resampling of the evaluation law, and fitted (reference-law) designs evaluated held-out that land within 3–7% of oracle.
- The paper is honest about what the experiments show, including that imputation/model-assisted estimation from complete source records matches branching ("information, not design, closes the gap"), and that position effects of 10% can bias both designs by 0.5–1.8 SE. The limitations section is substantive.

**Weaknesses**

1. [major] Practical significance for ML evaluation is thin and partly undercut by the paper's own results. The realized gain from within-record adaptivity is a factor 1.0–2.0 in records. The paper shows (A21, A23) that whenever complete source records exist, MI or regression imputation attains RMSE 0.88–1.00 of branching with nominal coverage even under the "other sex" shift, so the design-based capped estimators are only needed when no trusted complete reference exists and the conditional law does not transport, a case the paper never exhibits empirically. In ML settings (model updates, budgeted classifiers) complete training records essentially always exist, so the "model update" motivation in the title/abstract is stretched. Meanwhile Table 1's Fix/full column shows the cap itself costs 2–6x relative to full information, far more than non-adaptivity costs; the more relevant design question for a practitioner (relax the cap on a subsample, or use PPI-style model assistance) is treated only in passing (A25).

2. [major] Narrow scope of applicability. Identifiability requires Fourier degree deg(d) ≤ b, i.e., essentially probability-averaged depth-≤b tree ensembles. The paper's own certificates (Table 10) show thresholded forests, GBDT with logistic link, and MLPs have identification floors of 0.055–0.66 at b = 3, so the framework does not apply to most deployed models without a separate approximation step whose first-order constant is "not determined here". The experiments use median-binarized features with m ≤ 12 and depth-b forests of 20 trees trained on a few hundred records; "old" and "new" forests are independent retrainings rather than updates.

3. [major] Scalability is not demonstrated. The fixed program has O(C(m,b)2^b) variables and column generation for b = 4 already takes ~49 min per task at m = 9; b = 4 at m ≥ 9 and all of b = 5 were abandoned. Real questionnaires and clinical panels have dozens of items. "Larger menus need restricted masters with the same certificates" is asserted but not shown, so the exact computations are a diagnostic for toy-sized problems rather than a usable design tool.

4. [major] The submission is not self-contained. Several appendix sections (A12, A18.3, A25, A26, Table 9, Figure 4) report results on SUSHI, SAPA, "the ten binary tasks", "all 70 controlled tree tasks", "the eight-feature experiment" and a "larger-tree experiment", none of which is introduced in the submission; they are deferred to a "separate supplementary report" and there are references to "v12 results" and "earlier experiments". The main text's Section 8 quotes a quantitative limitation (position effects biasing by 0.5–1.8 branching SEs) that rests entirely on these undocumented experiments. A reviewer cannot verify these numbers.

5. [minor] The headline "up to 25 times as many respondents" is against completion-only path guessing, which the paper itself treats as a straw-man classical simulation; no practitioner would deploy it. The honest comparison (optimized fixed scores vs branching) is 1–2x.

6. [minor] The rounds lower bound (Theorem 3c) is informative only for b ≥ 10 (needs 2^{b-2} > b(2b+1)); for the caps actually studied (b = 2–6) the only known separation of two rounds from branching is the constant 4/3. The abstract's "two rounds can lose almost the whole exponent" is asymptotic in b and not relevant to the regime of the experiments.

7. [minor] Presentation is very dense: the abstract is one paragraph with roughly fifteen numbers; six named constants (V, W, M, Γ, N, K, Φ); "binding" is used before it is defined; cross-references are occasionally off ("Appendices A4–A3"; Section 6 cites A16 for the all-forms program while A16 describes a tree-path menu and A20 describes the all-forms program); Table 2's right-hand columns are ambiguous.

8. [minor] The assumption that labels are observed for every record and charged separately reverses the economics of active testing / PPI (labels expensive, features cheap). This should be stated up front since it defines the regime where the results matter.

**Soundness** 3 — The theorems I checked are correct and the computations are certified, but some quantitative claims in the main text rest on experiments not described in the submission, and the finite-sample constant (104) and the biased-regime constant are loose or open.

**Presentation** 2 — Precise but extremely dense; heavy notation, overloaded abstract, and an appendix that references datasets, tasks and versions absent from the paper make it hard for an ML reader to follow or verify.

**Significance** 3 — A clean, sharp theory of the adaptivity gap for capped-record estimation is a real contribution to information-constrained estimation, but the demonstrated practical gain is 1–2x in a narrow model class at toy scale and disappears when complete reference data exist.

**Originality** 3 — The question and the sharp constants are new; the tools (modulus-of-continuity duality, van Trees, path-guessing simulation, pointer chasing, augmented IPW) are established, as the authors acknowledge.

**Key Questions for Authors**

1. Can you exhibit a realistic setting (real or semi-synthetic) in which imputation / model-assisted estimation from complete source records fails (coverage collapses or bias exceeds the design gain) while the capped design-based scores remain valid? In A21/A23 the comparator wins under both targets. A convincing case would substantially raise my view of practical significance; absent one, I read the paper as a theory contribution whose applied story is limited to settings without any complete reference.

2. How do the fixed-form SOCP and the branching column generation scale to m ≈ 30–100 items and b ≈ 4–6 typical of questionnaires or lab panels? Can column generation (or a restricted master with certificates) be run on the fixed menu too, and have you tried any m ≥ 20 instance? A working demonstration at realistic scale would move the method from diagnostic to usable and would raise my recommendation.

3. For caps b ∈ {3,…,6}, can the optimal two-round (or r-round) design be computed exactly (column generation over A_r), and how much of the fixed/branching log-gap does optimal two-round adaptivity capture on the BRFSS trees? Table 3's "Two" column uses a heuristic (root question then best form). If optimal two rounds capture most of the gain, the practical message ("one skip is enough") would be much stronger than the asymptotic hierarchy.

4. Which experiments back the Section 8 numbers on position effects (0.5–1.8 SE), and will you either describe the SUSHI/SAPA tasks fully in the submission or remove every reference to undocumented tasks, "v12", "earlier experiments" and the external supplementary report? My soundness and presentation scores depend on the submission being verifiable on its own.

5. The bracket's lower end "never exceeds" the realized ratio on screening trees but 75/240 forest tasks realize a ratio below the reference-law lower end. What decision rule should a collector use, and what is its false-positive rate for recommending branching? A clear, validated rule would strengthen the claim that the gain "is detected in advance by the bracket".

**Limitations**

Yes, largely. Section 8, A25 and A26 discuss order effects, identification floors, the need for a reference, and cost regimes candidly. I would suggest (i) stating in the abstract/introduction that labels are assumed free and always observed and that the relevant regime is "no trusted complete reference", (ii) foregrounding that complete-record imputation closes the gap in all experiments reported, and (iii) stating explicitly that the exact computations were only feasible for m ≤ 12, b ≤ 6.

**Overall Recommendation** 3 — Weak reject. Clear theoretical merits (novel, sharp, correct results with certified computations), but the practical value for ML evaluation is modest and partly self-refuted, the applicable model class and problem scale are small, and the submission's appendix is not self-contained; together these outweigh the merits for an ICML main-track slot in its current form.
